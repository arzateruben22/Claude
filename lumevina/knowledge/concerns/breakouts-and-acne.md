## Habits and everyday tips you give

Habits & Everyday Skincare Tips — 

At Lumevina Aesthetics Spa, I educate clients on how their everyday habits can affect their skin, especially when treating acne, sensitivity, dehydration, and a compromised skin barrier. My approach focuses on consistency, proper hygiene, realistic lifestyle changes, and maintaining a personalized skincare routine.
1. Pillowcases & Bedding
- Change pillowcases regularly, ideally 2–3 times per week, especially if you're acne-prone or use heavy hair products.
- Keep bedding clean to minimize the buildup of oils, sweat, and product residue.
- Avoid heavily fragranced detergents and fabric softeners if your skin is sensitive or easily irritated.
- Clean towels should always be used when drying your face. Gently pat rather than rub.

2. Phones & Touching Your Face
- Regularly clean your phone screen, especially if it frequently touches your cheeks.
- Avoid touching your face unnecessarily or resting your face in your hands.
- Never pick, squeeze, or pop active breakouts. This increases the risk of inflammation, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, and scarring.
- Keep your hands clean before applying skincare products.


3. Hair Products & Hair Hygiene
- Be mindful of hair products containing heavy oils, waxes, and pomades, especially if you experience forehead, temple, or hairline breakouts.
- Keep hair away from your face when sleeping or exercising if products tend to transfer onto your skin.
- Rinse shampoo and conditioner thoroughly and cleanse your face after washing your hair to remove residue.
- Avoid allowing styling products to sit directly on acne-prone areas.
4. Makeup & Makeup Brushes
- Acne-prone clients should prioritize non-comedogenic, acne-safe makeup products whenever possible.
- Clean makeup brushes and sponges regularly to minimize buildup.
- Never sleep in makeup. Always properly remove makeup and sunscreen before bed.
- Double cleansing may be recommended when wearing makeup or water-resistant SPF.
- Avoid sharing makeup or applicators.
- Makeup can be worn while treating acne, but product selection and proper removal are important.

5. Diet & Hydration
- I encourage clients to maintain a balanced diet and stay properly hydrated.
- Certain dietary factors, including high-glycemic foods and some dairy products, may contribute to acne in certain individuals. However, everyone's triggers are different.
- I do not encourage restrictive diets or automatically tell clients to eliminate entire food groups.
- When appropriate, I recommend paying attention to potential dietary triggers and discussing significant concerns with a qualified healthcare professional.
- Drinking water supports general health, but it does not replace topical hydration or acne treatment.
6. Sleep & Stress
- Prioritize adequate, consistent sleep to support overall skin health and recovery.
- Stress can contribute to acne flare-ups in some individuals.
- I encourage realistic stress-management habits, including movement, rest, and maintaining a manageable routine.
- Acne is multifactorial, and clients should never feel that every breakout is caused by something they're doing wrong.
7. Workouts, Sweat & Exercise
- Exercise is encouraged, but proper post-workout hygiene is important.
- Avoid sitting in sweaty clothing or leaving sweat, makeup, and heavy products on the skin for extended periods.
- Gently cleanse the skin after workouts when possible.
- Avoid harsh scrubbing or over-cleansing, which can compromise the skin barrier.
- Use clean workout towels and avoid repeatedly rubbing the face.
- For clients receiving chemical peels, microchanneling, or other advanced treatments, follow the specific post-treatment exercise restrictions provided by their esthetician. Heated workouts, saunas, and excessive sweating may need to be temporarily avoided.

### 8. Sunscreen & Sun Exposure
- Daily SPF is one of my biggest non-negotiables.
- Apply a generous amount of broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every morning. I generally recommend approximately two finger-lengths for the face and neck, adjusting as needed for adequate coverage.
- Reapply every two hours while outdoors, and after swimming or sweating.
- Sun protection is especially important for clients treating acne marks, hyperpigmentation, or undergoing chemical peels.
- I remind clients that sunscreen is important even when it's cloudy.
- Clients undergoing microchanneling or certain advanced treatments must follow their specific aftercare instructions, which may temporarily restrict sunscreen application immediately after treatment.

### 9. Skincare Consistency & Skin Barrier Health
- Consistency is more important than using an excessive number of products.
- Follow the personalized morning and evening routine provided by your esthetician.
- Do not introduce multiple new active ingredients at once without guidance.
- More exfoliation does not mean faster results.
- Dryness, burning, persistent redness, and excessive peeling may indicate irritation or an impaired skin barrier.
- If irritation develops, contact me before continuing or increasing active ingredients.
- Barrier repair days may be incorporated into acne routines to allow the skin to recover.
- Acne treatments such as mandelic acid and benzoyl peroxide must be introduced and adjusted according to skin tolerance.
- Apply skincare in the recommended order, allowing products to absorb and dry as directed, particularly before applying stronger acne treatments.
- Avoid applying strong actives around the eyes, eyelids, corners of the nose, and lips unless specifically instructed.
- Never assume that an active breakout means your routine isn't working. Acne treatment requires time, consistency, and periodic adjustments.

### 10. Expectations & Professional Guidance
- Healthy skin is a long-term commitment, not an overnight transformation.
- Acne treatment often requires several months of consistency, depending on the type and severity of acne.
- Professional treatments and homecare work together. One cannot completely replace the other.
- Avoid constantly switching products based on social media trends.
- Products that work for someone else may not be appropriate for your skin.
- I encourage clients to communicate any changes, irritation, new medications, or concerns so their treatment plan can be adjusted safely.
- My philosophy is to educate clients rather than overwhelm them with unnecessary products or unrealistic expectations.

## When must this come to you, or to a doctor?

Acne that hasn't moved after months- a year of doing the recommendations and routine properly, breakouts that are clearly tied to your cycle, or anything that looks infected: that's when I bring in a dermatologist, and anything about medications is your doctor's.
