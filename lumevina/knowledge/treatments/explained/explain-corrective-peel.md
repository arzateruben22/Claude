# Corrective Peel: what to expect

## What happens, step by step

Double cleanser and prep, then a stronger or layered peel applied in stages while I watch how your skin responds, then neutralized, LED THERAPY, calmed and protective finishing products. You go home with specific products and instructions for the week.

## Does it hurt? How does it feel?

Hot and stingy for several minutes, more than the Refresh Peel; it eases as it neutralizes. I will fan you to ease the sensation. Your skin feels tight afterwards.

## How they'll look after, and for how long

Plan a week. Pink to red for a day or two, tightness, then visible peeling on days 2 to 5, done by day 7. No makeup for 24-48 hours, no sun, no heat. Not the week before an event.

## How often should they book it?

Every 4 to 6 weeks

A series of 3 to 4, then maintenance with facials or Refresh Peels.

## When will they see results, and how long do they last?

Once the peeling is done: smoother texture, fewer dark marks, softer fine lines and clearer skin, improving with each one in the series. Daily SPF is what keeps it.

## Who it's best for, and who should book something else

Texture, post-acne marks, sun damage, fine lines and stubborn congestion, on skin that's already used to actives. Sensitive, reactive or very dark-marking skin, and anything active or open, usually starts with the Refresh Peel.

## Who has to wait, or can't have it

Accutane in the last 6 to 12 months, a prescription retinoid in the last week, a sunburn or tan, active cold sores or breakouts, a recent laser or peel, pregnancy or breastfeeding, a named skin condition, or a keloid history: tell me first; several of these need your doctor's OK. Clients with Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI, or anyone prone to hyperpigmentation, typically require **4–6 weeks of skin preparation** before more intensive chemical peels. This may include tyrosinase inhibitors to help prevent pigmentation, daily broad-spectrum SPF 50, and a consistent skincare routine to maintain a healthy skin barrier. Preparation is customized based on skin type, pigmentation history, and peel strength to minimize the risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH). Reach out before hand.

## Anything to do before this one, beyond your general list

Pause retinoids and acids for a week, no waxing or dermaplaning on the face for a week, no sun, and plan the week after to be a quiet one for your skin.
