# Treatments, in general

General esthetics knowledge, not Evelyn's words. What a kind of treatment is and what's typical. Evelyn's menu descriptions say what her treatments include; her playbook says how often she books them and for whom.

## A professional facial

A cleanse (often double), exfoliation (enzyme or a gentle acid), steam or softening, extractions where needed, a mask aimed at the skin's condition, massage, and finishing products with SPF. Common additions: LED light, oxygen, high frequency, cold tools. A facial can't change skin in one visit; it clears, calms and resets, and the home routine does the work between visits.

**How often:** most estheticians suggest every four to six weeks (one skin cycle) for maintenance; every two to four weeks during an acne program or a corrective series.

## Enzyme vs. acid exfoliation

An **enzyme** (papaya, pumpkin) dissolves dead cells gently with little to no downtime. An **acid** exfoliation (a light peel) goes a little deeper for texture, tone and congestion. Which one is used depends on the skin that day.

## Chemical peels

Peels an esthetician performs are **superficial**: they work in the top layers to improve texture, tone, dark marks, fine lines and breakouts, and are repeated in a series (commonly three to six, two to four weeks apart). Deeper peels are medical.

- A **no-downtime peel** (light, "lunchtime"): slight pinkness for an hour or two, maybe light flaking on day two or three, makeup the next day.
- A **medium-depth esthetic peel** (stronger acids or layered): pink to red for a day or two, tightness, visible flaking or peeling on days two to five, and a week of gentle care. Not before a big event; plan a week.

Peels sting or feel warm while on the skin, usually for a few minutes, and are neutralized or self-neutralize. Some skin barely flakes and still benefits; peeling isn't the measure of a peel working.

## Dermaplaning

A sterile blade gently removes the top layer of dead skin and the fine vellus hair ("peach fuzz"). Painless; skin is smooth and makeup sits better right away. Slight pinkness for a few hours is normal. Hair grows back the same (not thicker or darker; that's a myth), usually in three to five weeks. Commonly done every four to six weeks and often paired with a facial or a light peel, which then penetrates better. Not usually done on active breakouts, open skin or very reactive skin.

## Microchanneling and microneedling (collagen induction)

Tiny, controlled micro-injuries in the skin trigger it to make new collagen and elastin, and let serums (often growth factors) absorb. Used for acne scars, texture, pores, fine lines and uneven tone. Microchanneling devices stamp straight down; microneedling pens drag and are generally deeper and more medical. Expect redness like a sunburn for a day or two, tightness, sometimes light flaking, and no makeup for about a day. Results build over weeks as collagen forms, and a series (commonly four to six sessions, four weeks apart) is where the change shows. Not done on active acne, open skin, cold sores, during Accutane, or on skin that scars with keloids without a doctor's OK.

## LED light

Red light calms and supports collagen; blue light targets acne bacteria. Painless, no downtime, cumulative; a nice finisher.

## Oxygen and cold tools

Oxygen infusions plump and brighten with no downtime. Cryo globes and cold rollers reduce redness and swelling after extractions or exfoliation.

## Extractions

Clearing blackheads and whiteheads by hand or with a tool, after the skin is softened. Mildly uncomfortable, worth it when congestion is there, and the reason some skin looks a little red or has a few small bumps for a day. Deep cystic bumps aren't extracted; they're calmed.

## Acne programs

Clearing acne is a process, not a facial: a treatment every two to four weeks (exfoliation, extractions, calming and antibacterial steps, often a peel or LED) plus a strict, slowly escalated home routine. Most programs count on 90 days to see a real difference and three to six months to be mostly clear, with consistency at home as the deciding factor. Purging in the first weeks is common.

## Consultations

A look at the skin, a review of the current routine and habits, and a plan: which treatment, which products and in what order. Virtual consultations work for routine questions; in-person is better when the skin needs to be seen up close.
