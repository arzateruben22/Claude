# Ingredients, in plain words

General esthetics knowledge, not Evelyn's words. What an ingredient does in general; never a claim about a specific product beyond its own label, and never a recommendation for a particular client (that's Evelyn's).

## Cleansers

- **Gel and foaming cleansers** lift oil well; usually for oily and combination skin, or a second cleanse.
- **Cream and milk cleansers** are gentler; usually for dry, dehydrated or sensitized skin.
- **Cleansing oils and balms** dissolve makeup and SPF; often the first step of a double cleanse at night.
- Cleansing twice a day is enough. "Squeaky clean" usually means stripped.

## Hydrators and moisturizers

- **Humectants** (glycerin, hyaluronic acid, sodium PCA, aloe) pull water into the skin. They work best on damp skin with a moisturizer over them; on their own in dry air they can leave skin feeling tighter.
- **Emollients and occlusives** (squalane, shea, oils, dimethicone, petrolatum) smooth and seal. Gel creams are lighter, for oily or acne-prone skin; creams and balms for dry.
- **Ceramides, cholesterol and fatty acids** are the skin's own barrier lipids; good when skin is sensitized or over-exfoliated.
- **Niacinamide (vitamin B3)** calms, helps oil balance and the barrier, and softens dark marks over time. Generally well tolerated and fine alongside most actives, including vitamin C (the "don't mix them" idea is outdated).
- **Peptides** signal the skin to make more collagen and support firmness. Gentle; usually fine with everything.

## Exfoliants

- **AHAs** (alpha hydroxy acids) loosen dead cells on the surface and, over time, help texture, tone and dullness. Glycolic is the smallest and strongest; lactic is gentler and hydrating; **mandelic** is the largest and gentlest, works more slowly, and is a common choice for sensitive skin, deeper skin tones and acne because it's less likely to cause irritation and dark marks, and it has some antibacterial action.
- **BHA** (salicylic acid) is oil-soluble, so it gets into pores: blackheads, congestion, oily skin. Usually 0.5 to 2% at home.
- **Enzymes** (papaya, pumpkin, pineapple) digest dead surface cells without acid; the gentlest option, common in facials.
- **Scrubs** (physical exfoliants) can work on body skin; on the face, harsh grains are a common cause of irritation and are usually skipped when acids or retinoids are in the routine.
- Too much exfoliation looks like: shine with tightness, redness, stinging, more breakouts, flaking. Less is more; most skin does well with acids a few nights a week, not every night.

## Acne actives

- **Benzoyl peroxide** kills acne bacteria and helps clear inflamed breakouts. It bleaches fabric (towels, pillowcases), can dry and irritate, and is usually started at a low strength (2.5% is often as effective as higher percentages with less irritation) for short contact or a few nights a week before building up.
- **Sulfur** absorbs oil and calms inflamed spots; a common spot treatment and mask ingredient.
- **Salicylic acid** and **mandelic acid**: see exfoliants.
- **Azelaic acid** helps breakouts, redness and dark marks; often well tolerated.
- **Hydrocolloid patches** draw fluid from a whitehead and stop picking; they do nothing for deep, closed bumps.
- **Retinoids**: see below. Over-the-counter retinol and retinal help acne by keeping pores from clogging; prescription retinoids (tretinoin, adapalene, tazarotene) are a doctor's call.

## Retinoids

- **Retinol** has to convert in the skin before it works; the gentlest, slowest, most widely used.
- **Retinal (retinaldehyde)** is one step closer to the active form: faster results than retinol, a little more potential for irritation, still available without a prescription.
- **Prescription retinoids** (tretinoin, adapalene, tazarotene) are stronger and are prescribed and managed by a doctor; they also change what an esthetician can safely do (see red flags and scope).
- All retinoids: start low and slow (two or three nights a week), buff over moisturizer if sensitive, expect some dryness or flaking in the first weeks, use SPF daily, and pause before exfoliating treatments and waxing. Not used in pregnancy or while breastfeeding without a doctor's say-so; most estheticians treat that as a hard stop.

## Brighteners

- **Vitamin C** (ascorbic acid and its derivatives) is an antioxidant that brightens, supports collagen and helps SPF do its job; used in the morning under sunscreen. Pure ascorbic acid can sting on a sensitized barrier.
- **Niacinamide, azelaic acid, tranexamic acid, kojic acid, licorice, arbutin, alpha-arbutin** slow pigment production; they work gradually (8 to 12 weeks) and only hold with daily SPF.
- **Hydroquinone** is a prescription-strength lightener in many places and a doctor's call.

## Sunscreen

- **Mineral** (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) sits on the skin and reflects and absorbs UV; usually the first choice for sensitive, acne-prone, post-treatment and pregnant clients. Can leave a cast on deeper tones; tinted versions help.
- **Chemical** (avobenzone, octocrylene and others) absorb UV; lighter textures, more likely to sting after a treatment.
- Either way: broad spectrum, SPF 30 or more, enough of it (about a quarter teaspoon for the face), reapplied every two hours in the sun. Makeup with SPF is a top-up, not the base.

## Fragrance and essential oils

Common triggers for stinging and redness on sensitized skin. "Unscented" can still contain masking fragrance; "fragrance-free" is the clearer label.

## Supplements

Supplements are outside an esthetician's lane. Whether to take, stop, mix or dose one is a question for the client's doctor or pharmacist, and for Evelyn to hear about, not for the chat to answer.
