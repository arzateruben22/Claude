# How skin works

General esthetics knowledge, not Evelyn's words. Use it to explain, never to diagnose or to decide what a particular client should do.

## Skin type vs. skin condition

- **Type** is what the skin tends to be: oily, dry, combination (oily T-zone, drier cheeks) or normal. It's mostly genetic and changes slowly, with age and hormones.
- **Condition** is what the skin is doing right now: dehydrated, sensitized, congested, breaking out, hyperpigmented. Conditions come and go, and products, weather, stress and habits move them.
- Any type can have any condition. Oily skin can be dehydrated; dry skin can break out.

## Dry vs. dehydrated

- **Dry** skin makes less oil. It feels rough or flaky most of the time and usually wants richer moisturizers.
- **Dehydrated** skin is short on water, not oil. It feels tight after cleansing, looks dull, shows fine lines that smooth out with hydration, and can be oily at the same time. Over-cleansing, over-exfoliating, heat, wind and dry air are common causes. Hydrating toners, humectants (glycerin, hyaluronic acid) and a moisturizer that seals them in usually help.

## The barrier

The outermost layer of skin, with its oils (lipids), keeps water in and irritants out. When it's worn down (too many actives, harsh cleansers, hot water, scrubbing, cold wind, sunburn), skin stings with products that used to be fine, looks red or shiny-tight, flakes, and breaks out more. A barrier recovers with fewer actives, a gentle cleanser, a moisturizer with ceramides or other barrier lipids, and SPF. Most "my skin suddenly hates everything" stories are a tired barrier.

## Turnover and why results take weeks

Skin renews itself in roughly four weeks when young, slower with age (five to six weeks or more). A product or treatment that works through turnover (acids, retinoids, acne routines, pigment correction) needs at least one full cycle to show, and usually two or three. That's why consistent home care over 8 to 12 weeks matters more than any single visit, and why a routine shouldn't be judged in a week.

## Skin tone and sensitivity to injury

Deeper skin tones (more melanin) are more likely to leave a dark mark after any irritation, breakout or over-aggressive treatment (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation). Gentler acids such as mandelic and lactic, careful peel depth, no picking, and daily SPF matter even more. Lighter skin tends to go red rather than dark. Neither is "better"; the aftercare rules just weigh differently.

## Oil, pores and what can't change

Pore size is set by genetics and oil flow. Pores can't be closed or shrunk, but they look smaller when they're kept clear of oil and dead skin (exfoliation, retinoids, clay or sulfur masks) and when the skin around them is firmer. Nothing "tightens" a pore for good.

## Hormones

Oil production rises with androgens, which is why breakouts cluster at puberty, around periods, with some birth control changes, with PCOS and in stressful stretches. Jawline and chin breakouts that come and go monthly are classic. Routines help, but when the driver is hormonal, a doctor is often part of the answer. That's a hand-off, not a product.

## Sun

UV is the main cause of early lines, uneven tone, dark spots and melasma, and it undoes pigment work. Daily broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher is the one thing every esthetician agrees on. After any exfoliating treatment or active, skin burns more easily for days to weeks.
