# Waxing, in general

General esthetics knowledge, not Evelyn's words. The usual rules before and after waxing; Evelyn's own say what she does.

## Before

- **Hair length:** about a quarter inch (6 mm), the length of a grain of rice: roughly two to three weeks of growth after shaving, or four to six weeks after a wax. Too short and the wax can't grip; very long can be trimmed, but not shorter than that.
- **Don't shave between waxes.** Shaving resets the growth cycle and makes the next wax patchier and more painful. Waxing gets easier with regular visits because the hair comes in finer and sparser.
- **Exfoliate gently** two or three days before (not the day of), and moisturize in the days before so the skin isn't dry or tight.
- **Day of:** clean, dry skin; no lotion, oil or deodorant on the area; loose, breathable clothing; some clients take an over-the-counter pain reliever 30 to 45 minutes before (their call, and their doctor's if they take other medicines).
- **Periods:** waxing is possible during a period (a tampon or cup for a Brazilian); skin is often more sensitive in the days before and during, so some clients prefer to book around it.
- **Sun:** no waxing on sunburned or freshly tanned skin, and no sun or tanning for 24 to 48 hours after; waxing lifts skin cells, so sun on top is a burn waiting to happen. Vacation timing: wax at least two or three days before the trip.
- **Retinoids, acids and Accutane:** waxing on skin thinned by a prescription retinoid (tretinoin, adapalene, tazarotene) or during and for months after Accutane can tear or lift the skin. The standard rule of thumb is to stop prescription retinoids on the area about a week before (over-the-counter retinol and acids, three to five days) and to wait six to twelve months after finishing Accutane. Any of that is a question for the client's doctor and for Evelyn, not for the chat to decide.
- **Not waxed:** broken, irritated, sunburned or infected skin; moles and skin tags (worked around); active cold sores (lip); varicose veins; a recent chemical peel, dermaplaning or laser on the same area.

## First Brazilian tips

Let the hair grow to the right length, exfoliate a couple of days before, shower that day, wear loose cotton, breathe out as each strip comes off, and know the first one is the most sensitive; it gets easier each time. It takes about 30 to 40 minutes. Tell the esthetician about any medications, skin issues or pregnancy first (pregnancy waxing is common but the skin is more sensitive; some clients clear it with their doctor).

## After (24 to 48 hours)

- No heat or sweat: workouts, hot showers, baths, saunas, hot tubs, swimming.
- No sun or tanning on the area.
- No tight clothing on the waxed area; for a Brazilian, loose cotton underwear and no sex for about 24 hours.
- No touching, no scented lotions, oils or deodorant on the area for the first day; a soothing, fragrance-free product or aloe is fine.
- No exfoliating for 48 to 72 hours.

## After that: preventing ingrown hairs

Starting two or three days after, exfoliate the area gently two or three times a week (a soft mitt or a gentle acid toner or pad; mandelic, lactic, glycolic or salicylic are common) and keep it moisturized. Ingrowns come from dead skin trapping new hair; exfoliation and no tight friction are the fix. Don't dig at an ingrown with tweezers; a warm compress and exfoliation let it surface, and a stubborn, painful or pus-filled one is a reason to message Evelyn or see a doctor.

## What's normal

- Redness, warmth and small red bumps (a histamine response around the follicles) for a few hours to a day, longer for the first wax or on sensitive skin.
- Mild tenderness.
- A few tiny white bumps a day or two later as follicles settle.

## What isn't

- Bumps that turn into pustules, spread or hurt (folliculitis, an infection of the follicle); lifted or torn skin; blistering; a rash with hives; a burn. Message Evelyn, and for spreading infection or a burn, see a doctor.

## How often, and does it get better

Every four to six weeks for most areas (brows, lip and underarms often every three to four). Regular waxing thins the hair and makes each visit quicker and less uncomfortable; shaving in between undoes that.

## Sugaring vs. wax

Sugaring is a paste pulled in the direction of growth, often chosen for sensitive skin; hard wax is applied warm and removed without strips, soft wax uses strips. Same preparation and aftercare for all three.
