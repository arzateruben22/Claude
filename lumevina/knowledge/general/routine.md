# Putting a routine together

General esthetics knowledge, not Evelyn's words. The general logic of a routine; Evelyn's own order and her product directions win whenever she has written them.

## The order of steps

Thin to thick, water-based before oil-based, treatment products on clean skin, sunscreen last in the morning.

**Morning:** cleanse (or just rinse, for dry skin) → toner or mist → serum (vitamin C, niacinamide, hydrating) → eye product → moisturizer → sunscreen.

**Night:** cleanse (double cleanse if wearing makeup or sunscreen) → toner → treatment (acid, retinoid, acne active) → serum → eye product → moisturizer. Spot treatments go on last, on the spot only.

A toner is optional; a hydrating one adds water, an exfoliating one counts as an active.

## Introducing something new

- One new product at a time, two weeks apart, so a reaction has a clear cause.
- Patch test behind the ear or on the jaw for two or three nights before using an active on the whole face.
- Actives start two or three nights a week and build up as the skin allows. A new sting or flaking means slow down, not push through.
- Expect the first few weeks of a retinoid or acid to include some dryness or small breakouts; judge a routine at 8 to 12 weeks, not 8 to 12 days.

## What generally isn't combined

- **A retinoid and an acid (AHA/BHA) the same night.** Alternate nights instead.
- **Two exfoliants at once** (two acids, an acid plus a scrub, an acid plus an exfoliating cleanser every day). Pick one.
- **Benzoyl peroxide and retinol together** can cancel each other and irritate; most routines use benzoyl peroxide in the morning and the retinoid at night, or alternate.
- **Pure vitamin C and an acid or retinoid in the same step**: usually vitamin C in the morning, actives at night.
- **Niacinamide and vitamin C** are fine together; that caution is outdated.
- **Any active on freshly treated skin** (after a peel, dermaplaning, microchanneling or a wax): wait the days the aftercare says.

## How much to use

A pea for a retinoid or acne medication across the whole face; a few drops of serum; a nickel of moisturizer; a quarter teaspoon of sunscreen for the face alone. More active isn't faster, it's just more irritating.

## Missing days

A few skipped days don't reset progress. Pick the routine back up at the same strength; a week or more away from a retinoid or acid, go back to a few nights a week first.

## Starting with less

A full routine isn't required to start. Cleanser, moisturizer and SPF are the base; one treatment product aimed at the main concern is the first addition. Adding everything at once makes a reaction impossible to trace.

## Using other brands alongside

Products from different lines can usually sit in the same routine. What matters is the ingredient overlap: two exfoliants, two retinoids or several actives at once are the problem, not the brand mix. Whether a particular client should keep a particular product is a question for Evelyn.

## How long things last (rough)

A 1 oz serum used once a day lasts about two to three months; a 2 oz moisturizer about two to three months; a 6 oz cleanser three to four months; a 2 oz sunscreen used properly on the face, about a month. Opened products are best used within six to twelve months; sunscreen and vitamin C lose strength faster when they're warm or in light.

## Traveling

Cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen and the one treatment product the routine is built around; leave the rest. Plane air is dry, so a hydrating mist and more moisturizer help, and sun exposure on vacation is the usual reason to go easier on actives for a week.
