OC Ultra · the store and its prices

Looks, never speed.

Built the way Lumevina's payments were: one payment engine with one place where Stripe plugs in, in test mode until the owner says live. It sells paint, wheels, driver styles and a tip. It never sells power, credits or chance.

Exit1

What stays free

Everything in the game today, and the free customisation the UI pass is adding. The launch is built on local trust, so a player who never pays shouldn't be able to tell a store exists, except for a Store button.

  • Every freeway, every real exit start, every mode (Free Drive, Speed Survival, Races, Time Trial) and every race course
  • The four cars, earned with credits by driving: Rossini GT (the starter), Rossini Brio, Rossini Corsa, Vela E
  • Each car's factory paint, wheels and interior
  • The free Garage palette: six paints and two rim styles per car. Paid paints sit on top of it, never replace or copy it
  • The three drivers, both presentations, five skin tones, their looks and watches
  • The three abilities: Clear Path, Focus Time and Locked In
  • The radio, the weather and time of day, Ultra Realism, run cards, the local leaderboard and the achievements
  • Visiting the on-foot hangout, Lot 57, whenever it ships

      Prices that survive the fee

      Stripe's standard US online card price is 2.9% + 30¢ per successful charge, with no setup or monthly fee; international cards add 1.5% and currency conversion 1%. The 30¢ is what decides our prices.

      $0.99 is out

      Stripe would keep a third of it. Whole dollars from $2 read as honest, and Apple has allowed rounded prices since its 2023 pricing change, so the same prices would work in an app later.

      A cart helps

      Three $3 packs in one checkout pay one 30¢: $8.44 kept instead of $7.84. Stripe's pay-what-you-want can't share a checkout, so the tip always gets its own.

      Easy refunds are cheaper

      A refund costs Stripe's fee, about 39¢ on $3. A card dispute costs $15 that isn't returned, plus another $15 to contest it (returned only on a win). One dispute wipes out about six $3 sales.

      Later, once each thing exists

      Nothing here is sold, pre-sold or promised before it's built, shown in the game and checked. Each is already in the catalog as "not on sale", so the paywall can be tested against it.

      Getting out like a Sims character

      The Lot 57 hangout

      The owner's idea of getting out of the car and going to Golden Road becomes Lot 57: our own fictional taproom and yard at the real spot on Orangewood Avenue in Anaheim. The real venue's name, sign and look are never used, and the store never says "Golden Road"; a real business appears only with a signed deal.

      Free to visit

      Get out, walk, sit, order from the alcohol-free menu, watch the simulated crowd. It's the most talked-about idea, so it should spread, not sit behind a paywall.

      Outfits, $3 each

      The people builder already has the garments. Two examples in the catalog: Lot 57 Weekend and Lot 57 Night Out.

      Maybe one expansion, about $8

      A second hangout with its own streets, only if it's big enough to be worth it. Lot 57 itself stays free.

      Why not yet It's a prototype: the game has no on-foot avatar and no way out of the car. No sale, no pre-orders, and no Founder perk tied to it. Crowds are simulated and labelled so; no live occupancy, no multiplayer claim.

      Cars

      Earnable first; sold only as a twin

      New cars arrive earnable by driving. A car may also be sold only if its numbers equal a car you can already earn: the example is the Rossini GT Spider, $5, an open-top body with exactly the GT's numbers, also in the showroom for credits. Paying skips the credits, never the level.

      Why not yet The body isn't built. And a bought car can't be sold back for credits (the garage pays 60% of a car's price), or dollars would turn into credits.

      Custom kits

      Body kits, $5 each

      The GT's Coast Aero kit, the Brio's Street kit, the Corsa's Track kit and the Vela E's Aero kit. Looks only: the drag and downforce numbers stay the same, and the store says so: "The Corsa drives exactly the same."

      Why not yet Kit parts have to be lofted onto each body and checked from the seat, hood and chase cameras: days of work, so after launch.

      Support, after Day 90

      The OC Ultra Club

      $3 a month (we keep $2.59) or $30 a year ($28.62): a club badge, a new club paint each month that you keep after cancelling, and early test builds. No gameplay perks and no extra credits.

      Why not yet Only after Day 90, about 50 buyers and a 12-month paint plan. California's automatic renewal law applies: price and billing shown before Join, an unticked box, a confirmation email, cancel online the same way you joined, a yearly reminder. A lawyer checks the notice windows first.

      Not now, maybe never

      Battle pass, ads, paid weather

      A battle pass needs new rewards every few weeks, and its "before the season ends" pull runs against the no-pressure rule; if one ever comes, it never expires and costs about $5 to $8. No ads at launch. No paid weather pass: rain changes grip, so it would sell gameplay.

      The rules

      From the owner's decision of 16 October 2026 and the store's terms draft. Nothing here is legal or tax advice; the points marked "verify at launch" move often.

      Cosmetics only

      • Never power: no car faster than one you can earn, no grip, handling, power, score, XP or credit boosts, no paid abilities or cooldowns.
      • Never the credits you earn by driving: no packs, no doublers, no club bonus. This also keeps the store out of Stripe's rules on virtual currency.
      • No loot boxes, mystery packs or gacha: you see the exact item, on your own car, before you pay. The FTC's $20 million settlement with Genshin Impact's maker (Jan 2025) was over loot boxes and hidden currency prices.
      • No premium currency: one price per item, in dollars.

      Selling honestly

      • Before checkout: the item on your car, the price in dollars, and "Looks only: it changes nothing about how any car drives or scores".
      • A confirm step for every purchase; no one-tap buys, no fake timers. The FTC's $245 million Epic order (2023) was over button layouts that caused unwanted charges.
      • "Licence", not "ownership": California's AB 2426 (since 1 Jan 2025) needs a clear notice before checkout. Next to Buy: "You're getting a licence to use these items in OC Ultra, not ownership of them." Never "own forever".
      • No account is ever locked over a dispute.

      13 and over to buy

      • A neutral question first, "What month and year were you born?", with no default and no hint of the cutoff, before anything is collected.
      • Under 13: no account and no store; only the "under 13" result stays on the device. The whole game stays free to play.
      • 13 to 17: with a parent's or guardian's yes, ticked at checkout.
      • An account holds an email, which COPPA treats as personal information. The amended rule took effect 23 Jun 2025, with compliance due 22 Apr 2026.

      Refunds, receipts, restore

      • Refund within 14 days if you haven't used the item in a drive. Accidental purchases and a minor's purchase without permission: any time. A refund takes the items back.
      • Until the in-game button exists, the terms point to a support email (TODO: the owner sets it).
      • Stripe emails a receipt for every payment and refund.
      • Items live on your account: sign in with the same email on any device and tap Restore purchases. In test mode they stay on the device.

      Where people buy

      • The web, including the installed PWA: no app store and no store cut. Stripe's hosted Checkout page takes the card, with Apple Pay and Google Pay, so card data never touches our code.
      • iOS, if a public app ever ships: the US storefront may link out to web checkout since 1 May 2025, but Apple's commission on those sales is still in court (Supreme Court review granted 30 Jun 2026). Decide on the day. Verify at launch.
      • Android: under the Epic v. Google injunction until 1 Nov 2027; link-out fees apply from 1 Dec 2026. Decide on the day. Verify at launch.

      Tax, today

      • California doesn't tax electronically delivered digital goods today. SB 122 taxes "digital products" from 1 Jan 2027 but excludes video games; whether in-game cosmetics count isn't settled. Check the CDTFA's final rules before then.
      • Other states only past their thresholds ($100,000 of sales in most), so no Stripe Tax at launch.
      • Whatever the store earns is taxable income. Keep records.

      Plainly

      At the low end the store pays for its domain and nothing else. In the middle it covers its own database, not the launch's optional costs. Only the high case clears the full optional list. A California LLC's $800 yearly tax is more than any case brings in. The owner has said profit isn't the goal; these numbers agree. The store is a way for people who like the game to support it, and a test of whether they will. The plan's calculator runs your own numbers.
