## 1. The ambassador guide

*Give this to every paid promoter with their agreement. It is short on purpose.*

### What you're promoting

**[Top Speed OC]** is a free driving game of Orange County's real freeways: the 5, 405, 22, 55, 57, 73, 91, 133, 241
and 261, every interchange, every real exit, from the driver's seat of a supercar of our own make. It runs in the
phone's browser (and as an app) with nothing to download beyond a 3 MB page. Your line: **"It's your freeway."**
Everyone in OC has an exit; the game starts at it.

What's true, and what to say:
- The roads, ramps, exit signs and street bridges are real, from OpenStreetMap through Overture Maps. The sun is
  where it really is over OC right now.
- Traffic and weather are **simulated**. There is **no live traffic, no live weather, no multiplayer**. Never say
  or imply any of those.
- Scores and leaderboards are **on the player's device only**. There is no online leaderboard yet.
- The cars are our own fictional makes (Rossini, Vela). Never call them a Ferrari or anything real.
- **Nothing in the game costs money** and there is nothing to buy.
- The hangout (Lot 57) is **our own fictional venue**; it is not any real taproom.

### The four rules (these are in your agreement)

1. **Say you're paid.** Every post, story, video or comment about the game starts with **#ad** or the platform's
   "paid partnership" label, visibly, not buried in hashtags. If someone asks in person, say you're an ambassador for
   the game. This is the law (the FTC's endorsement rule), not our preference.
2. **Never ask for stars or reviews.** Do not ask anyone to rate or review the game anywhere, and never offer
   anything for a rating. Ask people to *play it* and *share their run card*. App Store and Google Play ban
   rewarded ratings; so does the FTC.
3. **A game, not a dare.** Every post carries the line **"A game. Drive the real ones at the limit."** Never film
   or post real driving, real speeding, or anything that could read as encouraging it. No posts from behind a real
   wheel.
4. **Only true things.** Nothing live, nothing real-branded, nothing that doesn't exist yet (see the list above).
   If you're not sure something is true, ask before posting.

One strike on rules 1–3 ends the agreement.

### What you do each week

- **2 posts** on your own accounts (any format): your exit, your run card, a 20–30 second phone capture.
- **1 in-person session** (an hour): a car meet, a campus spot, a coffee shop or shop that has said yes. You bring
  your phone and a QR card; people scan, pick their exit, drive, and share a run card. Get permission from whoever
  runs the place first; never set up where you haven't.
- **1 short note** to us (a few lines): how many people played, what they asked for, what broke, what phones.
  Attach any Feedback reports players copied for you (Pause → Feedback in the game).
- **Your code.** Your invite code is **[CODE]**. Everyone you bring in types it on first launch. It's how your
  work is counted.

### What you get

- **[$X] a month** for **[N]** weeks, paid **[when]**, as an independent contractor (you'll invoice; a 1099 if the
  year's total reaches $600).
- Your name on the **Founders** screen in the game's credits.
- Early access to new freeways and the first look at the desktop version.
- **[Merch]** for you and to hand out.

### The run card

After any drive, **Share run** on the results screen makes a picture of the run (the road's shield, the score or
miles, top speed, the streak) with **#TopSpeedOC** on it. That picture is the thing to post. It's made on the phone
and nothing is uploaded by the game; posting it is the player's choice.

## 4. Privacy policy (draft, for a lawyer's read)

**[Top Speed OC] privacy policy** · effective [date]

- **What the game stores.** Your settings, your profile (a display name you choose, an avatar colour, in-game
  stats and fictional credits), your best runs and race times. All of it is stored on your device, in the browser's
  local storage or the app's own storage. None of it is sent to us.
- **What the game sends.** Nothing. The game makes no network requests of its own after it loads. [If the web
  version keeps loading its font from Google Fonts, say so here: "The web version loads a font from Google Fonts,
  which receives your IP address as any web request does"; the app version bundles the font and makes no such
  request.]
- **Sharing.** "Share run" makes a picture on your device; you choose whether and where to share it. "Feedback"
  shows a report about your device and the drive (browser, screen, graphics chip, frame rate, the road you were on);
  you choose whether to copy or send it, and to whom. It contains no name, no account and no location.
- **Location.** The game does not ask for or use your location. During the launch it may ask for a ZIP code so we
  know which parts of the county are playing; only the first three digits are kept, on your device.
- **Invite codes.** [If a code counter is used: "When you enter an invite code, the code alone is sent to our
  server and counted. No other information is sent."]
- **Children.** The game is not directed at children under 13 and collects no personal information from anyone.
- **Changes and contact.** [contact email]. Changes will be posted here with a new effective date.

## 7. The 30-second capture script (for creators)

1. Open the game on your phone; tap **Start on**, pick your freeway, pick **your exit** (2–3 s on the list).
2. Tap **Drive**. The first shot is the driver's seat with your exit's sign ahead. Hold 4 s.
3. Floor it; take the exit or blow past it. 10 s.
4. Switch to the chase camera (tap the camera button) for 5 s, then back to the seat.
5. Pause → **End drive** → **Share run**. Show the run card for 3 s.
6. Caption: your exit's name, **#TopSpeedOC**, **#ad** first if you're paid, and "A game. Drive the real ones at the
   limit."

Record with the phone's own screen recorder; sound on (the engine and the radio are part of it).
