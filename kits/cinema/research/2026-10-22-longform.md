## Brief: TikTok delivery

# TikTok delivery brief: 60 s to 3 min science explainers (9:16)

I checked this on 2026-10-22. The container's proxy blocked direct fetches of tiktok.com and of every third-party site (it returned 403). Every claim below therefore comes from search-engine excerpts of the URL cited next to it. I did not read any of these pages in full. "Unverified" means I found no source published by TikTok.

## Numbers we should design to

| Item | Design to | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 61–179 s (our pick: 75–120 s) | Over 60 s qualifies for Creator Rewards. The API lets every creator post 3 min. |
| Frame | 1080×1920, 9:16, 30 fps constant | API accepts 360–4096 px and 23–60 fps |
| Codec | H.264 in MP4, yuv420p, faststart | API recommends H.264 |
| Video bitrate | 10–12 Mbps (about 225–270 MB at 3:00) | Third-party guides only. TikTok's ad minimums are 516 kbps (auction) and 2.5 Mbps (reservation/TopView). |
| Audio codec | AAC-LC, 48 kHz stereo, 192–320 kbps | Unverified, common industry practice |
| Loudness | −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP | Unverified, the most common third-party figure |
| Safe area, organic posts | Keep clear: top 220, bottom 480, right 140, left 60 px | Third-party checker |
| Safe area, promoted as an ad | Keep clear: top 240, bottom 660, sides 120 px; button column from y = 840 | TikTok in-feed ad template, as reported by third parties |
| Hook | State the question and show the payoff image in 0–3 s | TikTok ads help |
| Frames to render | 1,800 per minute, 5,400 at 3:00 | Our pipeline at 30 fps |

## Length
- **Upload limits.** In-app recording is reported at 10 min. TikTok has only confirmed a *limited test* of 60-minute uploads ([TechCrunch, 2024-05-16](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/16/tiktok-upload-60-minute-videos)). Some guides say web uploads now allow 60 min and 30 GB; others say 30 min and 10 GB. Both claims are unverified ([lilachbullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/tiktok-video-size/), [renderforest](https://www.renderforest.com/blog/tiktok-video-specs-dimensions), 2026).
- **API limits.** The Content Posting API allows up to 10 min and 4 GB. Each account's real cap is the `max_video_post_duration_sec` field. All creators get 3 min; some get 5 or 10 ([Media Transfer Guide](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-posting-api-media-transfer-guide), [Query Creator Info](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-posting-api-reference-query-creator-info); undated, live in Oct 2026). A film of 3:00 or less can be posted everywhere.
- **Performance.**
  - Buffer studied 1.1M videos. Videos over 60 s got at least 43.2% more reach and 63.8% more watch time. Only 12.3% of videos ran past 1 min. This is a correlation, not proof of cause ([Buffer, 2025-03-17](https://buffer.com/resources/longer-tiktoks-get-more-views-data/)).
  - Opus Pro studied 1,449 videos. It found videos over 60 s are rare among viral hits, though it allows longer runs for educational content ([Opus, 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/how-long-should-a-tiktok-be-2026)).
  - Creator Rewards requires videos over 1 min. A view counts once someone watches at least 5 s ([Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/creator-rewards-program), undated).
  - I found no peer-reviewed data on how long science explainers should be.

## Picture
- **Formats.** The API accepts MP4, WebM and MOV, and H.264, H.265, VP8 and VP9. Frame rate must be 23–60 fps. The upload is sent in chunks of 5–64 MB ([developers.tiktok.com](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-posting-api-media-transfer-guide)).
- **Resolution.** One third-party guide says 4K is shown at 1080p, but I could not confirm this ([postfa.st](https://postfa.st/sizes/tiktok/video)).
- **Phone uploads.** App file-size caps are reported as 287.6 MB on iOS and 72 MB on Android. Both figures are unverified ([metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-video-length/)). Upload from the web or TikTok Studio to avoid them.

## Audio and loudness
- TikTok does not publish a loudness target, and I found no independent measurement.
- Third-party guides disagree:
  - Most say TikTok turns playback down to about −14 LUFS with a −1 dBTP ceiling, and adds its own compression ([mixingandmastering.ai](https://mixingandmastering.ai/blog/lufs-for-tiktok-and-reels)).
  - One says TikTok does not normalize in the feed, so a hotter master at −7 to −8 LUFS plays louder.
  - One recommends about −11 LUFS ([trackgleam, 2026](https://trackgleam.com/learn/master-for-tiktok-reels-shorts)).
- **What this means for us.** The pilot was mixed to −18 LUFS. That will probably play about 4 dB quieter than the videos around it, because platforms usually turn loud audio down rather than quiet audio up. Master to −14 LUFS and −1 dBTP.
- **How to check.** Upload a private draft, screen-record it playing on a phone, and measure the recording ([mrvocal](https://mrvocal.com/posts/loudness-for-shorts)). Add this test to qa.py.

## Safe zones at 1080×1920
- **Organic posts.** Keep 220 px clear at the top, 480 px at the bottom (caption, username, sound) and 140 px on the right for the buttons ([revid](https://www.revid.ai/tiktok-safe-zone)).
- **In-feed ad template.** Keep 240 px clear at the top, 660 px at the bottom and 120 px on each side. The button column starts at y = 840. Its width is reported as 180 px by one source and 300 px by another ([socialync](https://www.socialync.io/tools/safe-zones/tiktok), read 2026-10-11; [upload-post](https://www.upload-post.com/tools/safe-zone-checker/), checked 2026-09-23).
- **AdKit's figures (strictest).** AdKit measured ad previews and reserves 242 px on the right and 707 px at the bottom ([adkit](https://adkit.so/tools/safe-zones/tiktok), Mar 2026).
- **Our rule.** Keep all text and science labels within x 120–900 and y 240–1260, so the film also works if it is promoted as an ad.

## Captions
- **TikTok's auto-captions.** Creators add them in the editor and can edit the text. Viewers can switch them off ([TikTok accessibility](https://www.tiktok.com/accessibility/en); [newsroom, 2021](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/introducing-auto-captions)). Captions on by default were reported, but the date is unclear ([SMT](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-announces-all-videos-auto-captions-default/696632/)).
- **Burned-in text.** TikTok's ad guide recommends on-screen text so the story works with the sound off, at 5–10 words per second ([ads creative guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/guides/what-is-ad-creative-guide)).
- **Our rule.** Burn in the key terms and short captions, which our accessibility floor needs anyway. If full captions are burned in, leave TikTok's auto-captions off so the text doesn't appear twice.

## Hook and retention
- TikTok says to introduce the main point in the first 3 s and land the hook within the first 6 s ([Creative Best Practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)).
- TikTok says 63% of the ads with the highest click-through rate showed the key message in the first 3 s. It also notes that music above 120 BPM tends to raise view-through rate ([Auction tips PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf), about 3–4 years old).
- **Our recommendation (not sourced).** Start on the payoff image at frame 0, with no logo card and no fade in from black. Add a fresh visual question every 15–20 s.

## Music licensing
- Business accounts see only the Commercial Music Library (CML). CML tracks are cleared for commercial use, but **only on TikTok**, and "Premium" tracks may cost extra. The general music library and trending sounds are not cleared for brands ([CML help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library?lang=en), [CML terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/commercial-music-library-user-terms/en)).
- Business terms require either CML or music whose rights you have cleared yourself ([Business Terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/business-terms-eea/en)).
- **What this means for us.** The studio's own synthesized score is owned outright and can also go to Reels and YouTube. Use it for client work.

## Labelling AI-generated content
- A label is **required** for AI-made or heavily edited content that shows realistic people or scenes, including AI audio that imitates a real person.
- A label is **not required** for artistic styles such as cartoons and anime, for minor edits, or for generic text-to-speech that doesn't sound like a recognizable person.
- A label doesn't affect distribution and can't be removed after posting. Unlabelled realistic content may be removed ([Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/ai-generated-content-label); [Community Guidelines 2025H2](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en-GB/integrity-authenticity?cgversion=2025H2update)). I could not confirm whether the August 2026 reissue changed this.
- TikTok labels a video automatically if it carries C2PA Content Credentials ([newsroom, 2024](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/partnering-with-our-industry-to-advance-ai-transparency-and-literacy)). A text-to-speech vendor that embeds these could trigger the label.
- **Code-rendered three.js animation and numpy audio are not AI-generated, so they need no label.**
- **EU rule.** From 2026-08-02, EU AI Act Article 50 requires disclosure of deepfakes, and providers must mark synthetic audio so machines can detect it ([Sidley, 2026-06-24](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/24/eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-preparing-for-compliance-by-2-august-2026/)). Never clone a real person's voice.
- **Our recommendation.** If we use a synthetic narrator, say "voice: synthetic" in the post caption, even though it isn't required.

## Brief: Our audio engines

Audit of the two audio engines. I changed nothing in the repo. Below, MWC is films/made-with-claude/src/audio.py and SS is films/skin-school-pilot/audio/score.py. Benchmark script: /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Claude/b65d3a2f-52c6-526e-b15b-5f369330bc42/scratchpad/longform/bench.py

**1. Voices and effects**
- **MWC.** The tonal voices are built on `saw_additive` (l.83), an additive saw with up to 60 harmonics.
  - Music: kick l.108, clap l.116, hat l.125, snare l.131, bass l.138, pluck l.145, pad l.151, FM bell l.159, 8-bit blip l.165.
  - Foley: whoosh l.174, riser l.186, impact l.195, shatter l.202, zap l.218, thud l.226, boing l.234, key_click l.241, shimmer l.248, coo l.260, tick l.273, and a crash written inline at l.394.
  - DSP: fft_filter l.47, stft_shape l.60, band l.77, adsr l.100, reverb l.297.
- **SS.**
  - Voices: sine pad l.66, FM glass l.73, tap l.79, fizz l.86, plop l.92, splash l.98, swell l.104, sine tick l.110, and a whistle written inline at l.152.
  - DSP: lp l.35, env l.57, reverb l.115.

**2. How cue times get from the picture to the audio**
- **MWC** reads timeline.json when it loads (l.21).
  - It takes duration and bpm from it (l.23-25), the chords through chord_at (l.310), and the foley times from mascot, search, toolbarPress, streak, viewer, keyframes and shards (l.332-445).
  - It writes -cues.json (l.479), which qa.py sync (l.253) and cue_spectrogram.py (l.56) read.
  - The picture reads the same JSON (main.js l.29).
- **What still breaks in MWC if the picture changes:**
  - Hard-coded times and notes: the quieter-arp window 6.0-9.5 (l.387), the end bells and bass at 11.0 (l.405-407), and the arp and chirp notes (l.328, l.340).
  - The zap's lead time is set in four places that disagree: audio.py −0.075 (l.438), fx.js beam −0.08 (l.207), and qa.py −0.03 (l.264, out of date) and −0.08 (l.287). qa-pass3.json already reports a zap placement error of −45 ms.
  - The matched-filter sync check (finds each cue's waveform in the effects stem) covers only land, zap, impact, coo and key (l.256-270). The other five cue kinds are never checked.
  - The groove assumes one section and counts bars from the drop (l.353).
  - A 16th note at 120 BPM is 3.75 frames, so a sound can come up to 25 ms before its frame.
- **SS** copies every time by hand from scenes3d.mjs. The conversion from scene time to film time is in comments (l.130-165), and one easing curve is inverted by hand (l.162). There is no cues file and no sync check, so any picture edit puts the sound out of sync without warning.

**3. Mix and master chain**
- **MWC buses:** drums, bass, music, sfx (l.319).
- **Kick sidechain:** gain 1−0.55·exp(−dt/0.11). That is about −7 dB on bass and about −3 dB on music (l.448-454).
- **Reverb:** one per bus, with no sends: music 2.2 s at wet .28, sfx 1.1 s at .16, drums 0.8 s at .08, bass dry (l.455-457).
  - The impulse is seeded noise with decay time = length/6.9, 15 ms predelay and a 7 kHz low-pass. The wet signal is scaled ×2.2 (l.297-307).
- **Mix bus, in order:**
  - Outro dip of −3 dB over 1.2 s (l.459-463).
  - High shelf of −3.5 dB from 4.5 to 9 kHz (l.465-469).
  - tanh(1.15x)/1.15 saturation (l.471).
  - 120 ms fade.
  - Peak normalize to −1.5 dBFS (l.474-475).
  - 16-bit write with no dither (l.484).
- **Stem:** the sfx stem is taken before the shelf and the tanh (l.477), so it does not add up to the mix.
- **Normalization:** render.cjs runs a two-pass ffmpeg loudnorm with I=−14, TP=−1, LRA=9 in linear mode (l.108-118), then encodes AAC at 256k (l.126).
- **QA:** ebur128 must land within 1 LU of target with true peak at most target +0.3 (qa l.316-326). A cross-correlation checks the mux offset (l.303-314).
- **Delivered file:** −13.7 LUFS, −4.7 dBTP.
- **SS:**
  - Chain: bed and fx buses (l.128), a 3.2 s hall at .35 and a 1.6 s room at .22, a 600 ms fade, tanh(1.1x)/1.1, and a peak normalize to −3 dBFS (l.166-169).
  - It has no loudness stage. The "−18 LUFS" in the README (l.103) is a side effect of that peak normalize.
  - build/ln.json shows input −18.12. In that pass loudnorm ran in dynamic mode and output −17.21.

**4. What both engines duplicate**
- Helpers: hz, db, rng and tvec (MWC l.29-42, SS l.19-32).
- `Bus` (l.280 vs l.44). SS truncates the start time and cuts the signal wrong if t<0.
- `fft_filter` vs `lp`. SS does not zero-pad, so filter tails wrap around to the start.
- `reverb`: same algorithm, but decay, damping, wet gain and predelay differ.
- Same sounds under different names:
  - bell vs glass (FM ratio 3.5 vs 2.76).
  - kick, thud and plop are the same pitch-swept sine.
  - whoosh vs whistle.
  - adsr vs env.
- Two different functions named "tick".
- Both engines repeat the saturation, fade, peak normalize and 16-bit write.

**5. What is missing for 1-3 minute films**
- **Song form:** there are only intro, drop and outro times (timeline l.135-139), and the groove is a single loop (l.352-389). We need sections with energy, layers and fills.
- **Tempo map:** there is one bpm (l.25), and the beat-grid check assumes it (qa l.293).
- **Stems:** we need dialog, music and effects stems taken after the master gain, so they add up to the mix.
- **Voice:** none today. We need to import a WAV per line, run a voice chain (high-pass, de-ess via stft_shape, compression), and detect onsets for captions.
- **Ducking under a voice:** the music should dip on a schedule taken from the voice line times: −9 dB, 80 ms attack, 350 ms release, 150 ms pre-roll. It also needs a 2-4 kHz cut on the music while the voice speaks.
- **Transitions:** nothing handles scene boundaries yet (crossfades, risers on cuts, key changes).
- **Limiter:** there is none, and loudnorm's linear mode falls back to dynamic when the true peak limit is hit.
  - Build it in numpy without scipy: 4× FFT upsampling in blocks, peak detection every 32 samples, 5 ms lookahead, release computed at that control rate, then the gain interpolated back to audio rate.
  - Add a numpy loudness meter to the BS.1770 standard (K-weighting applied as an FFT magnitude, gated 400 ms blocks).
  - ffmpeg ebur128 stays the final check.
- **Render time (measured):** MWC takes 10.8 s to make 12 s of audio, and 82% of that is `saw_additive`. At 180 s:
  - one 4-note pad takes 147 s;
  - the kick-duck loop scales with kicks × samples: 60 kicks take 15.6 s, so about 94 s for 360;
  - each reverb takes 8.4 s;
  - estimate: 6-8 minutes per pass on one core.
  - Fixes:
    - use wavetables instead of additive synthesis;
    - render a bar once and repeat it;
    - duck only in a short window after each kick;
    - use float32;
    - render sections in parallel with a ProcessPool;
    - cache each section by a hash of its slice of the cue sheet.

**6. Proposed module split: kits/cinema/studio**
- core.py: `SR`; `hz(n)`; `db(g)`; `rng(tag)`; `tvec(d)`; `Bus(n).add(sig, t, gain_db=0, pan=0)`; `read_wav(p)`; `write_wav(p, x, bits=24)`
- dsp.py: `fft_filter(x, lo, hi, slope=2)`; `stft_shape(x, gain_fn, hop=256, win=1024)`; `env(n, a, d, s, r)`; `reverb(x, seconds, wet, tag, predelay=0.015, damp_hz=7000)`; `upsample4(x)`; `lufs(x) -> (I, short_term)`; `true_peak(x)`
- instruments.py: `osc(f, dur, shape, cutoff)`; pad, pluck, bass, `bell(f, dur, ratio)`, kick, snare, clap, hat; `INSTRUMENTS` registry
- foley.py: `FOLEY = {kind: fn(event) -> ndarray}`; `LEAD = {kind: seconds}`, the single lead-time table used for placement and QA and exported to JS
- tempo.py: `TempoMap(segments).sec(beat)`, `.beat(sec)`, `.snap(sec, div=4)`
- compose.py: `arrange(sheet, tempo, palette) -> dict[str, Bus]`; `transition(kind, t, dur)`
- voice.py: `place(lines) -> Bus`; `chain(x)`; `onsets(x)`; `duck_curve(spans, n, depth_db=-9, attack=0.08, release=0.35, preroll=0.15)`
- master.py: `mix(buses, cfg) -> (mix, stems)`; `master(x, lufs=-14, tp=-1.0)`
- qa.py: `sync(cues, stem, FOLEY, LEAD, fps)`; `loudness(mp4)`; `stems_sum(stems, mix)`; `spectrogram(wav, cues, out)`
- render.py: `python3 -I kits/cinema/studio/render.py cues.json OUTDIR` writes mix.wav, stems/{dialog,music,effects}.wav and cues.json

The timeline or scene writes the cue sheet; it is never copied by hand. One format:
```
{"duration":92,"fps":30,"sr":48000,
 "tempo":[{"t":0,"bpm":96},{"t":48,"bpm":104}],
 "palette":"glass-lab",
 "sections":[{"id":"hook","t0":0,"t1":6,"energy":0.8,"chords":["Bm7","G","D","A"],"layers":["pad","pluck","kick"],"out":"riser"}],
 "events":[{"t":2.0,"kind":"impact","gain":-3,"pan":0,"tag":"drop"}],
 "voice":[{"id":"vo03","t":14.2,"file":"vo/03.wav","text":"TODO","duck_db":-9}],
 "master":{"lufs":-14,"true_peak":-1.0}}
```
`palette` is the vibe preset: instruments, reverb sizes, tilt and saturation. Times are in seconds, or "bar.beat" read through the tempo map.

## Brief: Rendering 60–180 s

Nothing was rendered, and nothing in /home/user/Claude was changed. The only thing I created is the empty scratch folder /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Claude/b65d3a2f-52c6-526e-b15b-5f369330bc42/scratchpad/longform. Long films are workable on this machine: about 3–4 h for 60 s and about 9–11 h for 180 s at 1080×1920. Getting there needs per-scene chunks, crash-safe resume and a beat sheet that compiles to the timeline.

**1. Measured cost and projections** (software rendering, 4 cores, no GPU)
- **made-with-claude, first full render** (`full1b.log`/`full1c.log`, S5–S6, K avg about 6, where K is the number of motion-blur sub-frames): 10.2 s/frame per worker at 16:9 and 11.5 s at 9:16. With 2 workers that is 5.1 and 5.8 s/frame of wall time.
- **Same render, whole film including the fast moves:** about 6.3 s wall at 16:9 and 7.3 s at 9:16.
- **Final-pass rules** (`pass3/16x9/render-log.json`, K up to 64, K avg 14.3): 36 s per worker, 17 s wall. This run overlapped a Skin School render, so these numbers are inflated. Calm shot S5 took 14 s per frame per worker; the Breakout shot S2 took 85 s.
- **What drives cost:** per worker, seconds ≈ 3 + (1.1 to 2) × K. The K rules are in `main.js:501-509`.
- **Skin School** (1280×720 / 720×1280, one page): 3.0 and 3.1 s/frame (`render-*-liq.log`). When another render ran at the same time this rose to 4.8 and 5.6 s, so two renders must never run at once.
- **Preview quality at 960×540** (`prof/`): 1.1–4.6 s per worker, about 2 s once warmed up.
- **PNG size:** 2.25–2.33 MB per frame at 1080p, 0.59 MB at half size.

Projected wall time at 1080×1920, 2 workers, nothing else running:

|---|---|---|---|
| 30 fps calm explainer (5.8 s/frame) | 2.9 h | 4.4 h | 8.7 h |
| 30 fps mixed moves (7.3 s/frame) | 3.7 h | 5.5 h | 11 h |
| 30 fps action, final-pass rules (about 11 s, estimate) | 5.5 h | 8.3 h | 16.5 h |

| Animatic: finish turned off, 6 fps (about 0.5 s/frame, from the Skin School README) | 3 min | 4.5 min | 9 min |

A Skin School-style scene at 1080×1920 has not been measured. Scaling by pixel count suggests about 3.5–4 s wall per frame, or about 5.5 h for 180 s.

**2. What breaks at 1–3 minutes**
- **Crashes:** 5 of about 11 runs died with "Target page… closed" after 90–270 frames (`full1.log`, `full1b.log`, `pass2/*.log`, `pass3/render-9x16b.log`). `render.cjs:88-97` keeps one page per worker for the whole run, and one failure kills every worker. A 5,400-frame render needs per-frame retry on a fresh page, and a new page every ~300 frames.
- **The per-frame log is only saved at the end** (`render.cjs:99-101`), so a crash loses it. pass2 has no log at all.
- **Resume only checks that the file exists** (`render.cjs:154-158`). A PNG cut short by a kill counts as done. Nothing records which code and timeline made a frame, so resuming after an edit mixes versions. `signatures.cjs --diff` can already list the frames an edit changed.
- **Hard cuts:** the shutter only stops at the end of the film (`main.js:309, 524`), and K takes the maximum of neighbouring frames (`main.js:503`). Every hard cut inside one long timeline would blend two shots and push K to 64. `cinema3d.js:187-188` stops at the scene's end, which is correct only if each scene is its own page.
- **One-off rules for this film live in the engine:** the shard and streak K floors (`main.js:508-509`) and the poster frame 68 (`render.cjs:129`).
- **Encode is all or nothing** (`render.cjs:170-171`). It runs two slow x264 passes over every PNG (`render.cjs:122-128`). `-shortest` (`render.cjs:126`) silently cuts the film if the voice or score runs longer.
- **Skin School `render.mjs`:**
  - Frames are piped straight to ffmpeg (line 199) with nothing on disk, so there is no resume.
  - One page per aspect renders everything in sequence (line 157).
  - `samples.push([i, hashes[i]])` (line 197) looks up a frame number in a list numbered by position, so the hashes are wrong whenever the render starts mid-scene (`--from`).
  - `--from` without `--to` still encodes and overwrites the full mp4 (line 184).
  - Contact-sheet frames are held in memory and inlined into one page (lines 198, 222).
- **Disk:** 2.25 MB × 5,400 frames = 12.2 GB per format per pass. 23 GB is free, and `made-with-claude/build` already holds about 4 GB of old passes.
- **Hand-keying:** the 12 s film needed 18 camera keys for each of 2 formats, plus 13 mascot moves, 7 chords and 11 viewer cues, all in absolute seconds (`timeline.json:40-150`). Skin School used 15 camera and 10 focus keys in 26 s (`scenes3d.mjs:168-209`). At 180 s that is about 270 camera keys per format, and inserting 2 s at 0:40 means shifting every later time by hand.
- **Determinism checks:**
  - Checking every 15th frame (`render.mjs:197`) means 360 re-renders, about 35 min per aspect. Check about 5 frames per scene instead.
  - Record the Chromium build, because the software renderer's output depends on its version.
  - Keep `Math.random` out of `seek()`. `render.mjs:158` seeds it once per page, so a page resumed mid-scene would produce different frames. Nothing uses it today.
- **QA:** `qa.py:62` reads every frame, which is fine at this length, but its checks only fit the Breakout film. Contact frames cost 26 s per frame per worker (`contact.log`), against about 10 s when rendered in order.

**3. Authoring 1–3 minutes without hand-keying**
- **Beat sheet that compiles to timeline.json.** Beats carry durations, not absolute times, so an insert pushes everything after it along. Example:
  ```
  {"beats":[{"id":"mouth","vo":"Every pore is a tiny well…","shot":"macroPush","subject":"pore","labels":["sebum"],"focus":["hair","gland"]},
            {"id":"clog","dur":"vo+0.6","shot":"craneBack","subject":"slot","events":["plugFall"]}]}
  ```
- **Shot library:** functions that take the subject's bounding box, the duration and the aspect, and return `keys.js` keys. Moves: establish, push-in, pull-back reveal, crane, orbit, follow (`rel`/`track`), macro slide, rack focus, whip. The 9:16 framing comes from `portraitScale`/`pfov`/`pl` instead of a second hand-keyed track.
- **Science primitives** in a new `kits/cinema/sci/`:
  - Particles and Brownian motion.
  - Cells (`metaballs.js`) and fluids or membranes (`liquid2d.js`).
  - Instanced ball-and-stick molecules.
  - Flow arrows.
  - Callouts with leader lines tied to 3D points, checked by `render.mjs`'s AUDIT.
  - Graphs that draw on, counters and scale bars.
- **Each primitive provides:** `build(opts, seed)`; a pure `seek(t)` with physics pre-baked and never re-simulated from 0 on each seek; `anchors()` for focus and labels; `events()` for sound cues; K-floor ranges.
- **One event list for picture and sound:** the compiled list also drives the master studio (score, foley, voice, captions), so picture and sound cannot drift apart.

**4. Chunked render plan and disk budget**
- **Unit:** one scene of 5–20 s on its own page, with its own duration so blur stops at cuts, × format × tier.
- **On disk:** `build/<tier>/<fmt>/<scene>/%05d.png`, plus `manifest.json` (code and timeline hash, Chromium version) and `frames.jsonl` appended after every frame.
- **Crash-safe writes:** write each PNG under a temp name, then rename it. On restart, skip only frames that have both a PNG and a log line, and retry a failed frame on a fresh page.
- **Tiers:**
  - T0 animatic, to lock the voice timing.
  - T1 draft, to review motion.
  - T2 final-look stills, one per 2 s: about 90 frames, about 20 min for a 180 s film.
  - T3 final, overnight, re-rendering only the frames `signatures.cjs` marks as changed.
- **When a scene finishes:**
  1. Check the frame count and hash 5 frames.
  2. Encode a high-quality intermediate (x264 crf 15; the current master measures 49.8 Mbps).
  3. Delete that scene's PNGs.
- **Final assembly:**
  1. Join the scene intermediates without re-encoding.
  2. Add the mastered audio and check that video and audio lengths match within one frame (no `-shortest`).
  3. Make the delivery encode. crf 19 runs 19.5 Mbps, about 440 MB at 180 s. TikTok re-encodes uploads, so 8–12 Mbps is enough.
- **Disk, 180 s at 9:16:**
  - At most 1.4 GB of PNGs in flight (one 20 s scene).
  - 1.1 GB of intermediates.
  - 0.2–0.45 GB delivery file.
  - About 3 GB in total, against 12.2 GB if every PNG is kept.
  - A 16:9 version needs the same again.
- **Workers:** keep 2, and run only one render at a time.

## Word timings
- **Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 (int8, through sherpa-onnx 1.13.8)** is a speech-to-text model that returns a time for each word.
  - Zero word errors on everything tested.
  - 0.6 s for an 8 s clip; a 4.4-minute file took 44 s in one pass and about 2.8 GB of memory.
  - Its times sit on an 80 ms grid, and sentence starts land **95–260 ms early** (checked against phrases placed at known times).
- **Fix (about 15 lines of numpy):** if a word's start lands in silence, move it to the first frame with sound.
  - Phrase starts then land within 1–10 ms on clean audio.
  - On a simulated phone recording (room echo plus noise) they land within 15–31 ms, under one frame at 30 fps.
- **Numpy-only aligner** (split at pauses, spread words by syllables):
  - Phrase starts are within 0–21 ms.
  - Words inside a phrase are off by about 150 ms on average (up to 370 ms).
  - Good enough for line-level cues, not for word-by-word captions.
- **Piper's own timings** (piper 1.8 `include_alignments=True`, needs the `onnx` package): word starts come straight from the voice engine, within about 30 ms. espeak merges "from the" into one word, so I had to split those back apart.
- **Kokoro** returns audio only, with no timings. Make it one line at a time so line starts are exact, then use Parakeet plus the fix for words.
- **Didn't work or fell short:**
  - whisper tiny.en runs, but gives no word times here and heard "pore" as "pour".
  - faster-whisper installs, but its model download is blocked.
  - pywhispercpp installs, but its models are on the blocked Hugging Face.
  - aeneas fails to build on Python 3.13.

## Loudness and mastering
- ffmpeg 6.1.1 has what we need: loudnorm, ebur128 with true peak, sidechain ducking, compressor, limiter, de-esser, noise reduction, rubberband time-stretch and libass captions.
- **Gotcha:** two-pass loudnorm straight to -14 LUFS quietly switched to its dynamic mode (the clip needed +9.7 dB and would have clipped).
- **Fix tested:** add gain, then a limiter running at 4x sample rate, then two-pass loudnorm with `linear=true`. Result: -13.8 LUFS, -2.2 dBTP, linear mode. Mixing and mastering a 12 s clip took 1.3 s. An AAC export at 192 kbps held -14.0 LUFS.
- Rubberband stretched a clip to 0.9x in 0.17 s, enough to fit a line to a shot.
- **Captions:** word-highlighted captions burned into 1080x1920 took 3.5 s for 12 s (`out/caption_test_9x16.mp4`). Captions drawn inside the three.js scene keep the client's own fonts; if we use libass instead, point `fontsdir=` at the repo's fonts.
