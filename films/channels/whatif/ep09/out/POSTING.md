A rope round the Earth | Possibility Lab Season 2 · Episode 3

**Description**

```
How much more rope? Round the whole Earth, lifted one meter off the ground.

A rope hugs the Earth, about 40,000 km long. Lift it one meter above the ground, everywhere. A circle's length is 2 × π × its radius, so lifting it a meter adds 2 × π × 1 m: about 6.3 meters of rope, about four of you lying head to toe. The planet's size cancels out: only the lift matters, and ten meters higher needs about 63 meters.

Series: Season 2, episode 3 of 6.

Sources
1. The rope round the equator is about 40,000 km: pi x 12,756 km = 40,074 km. — NASA: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/
2. Circumference = 2 x pi x radius (a mathematical definition). — Derivation (no outside page; arithmetic shown in the film)
3. Lifting the rope evenly by 1 m raises the circle's radius by 1 m (idealised: a perfect sphere, a smooth rope). — Derivation (no outside page; arithmetic shown in the film)

5. 6.28 m / 1.7 m (an adult's height, assumption) = 3.7: 'about four of you'. — Assumption (no outside page; arithmetic shown in the film)

7. extra = 2 pi h: the planet's radius is not in the answer. — Derivation (no outside page; arithmetic shown in the film)
```
