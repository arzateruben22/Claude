**Description**

```
Could lightning power the world? Catch every bolt, for a whole year.

About 44 flashes a second, over a billion a year, each carrying roughly a billion joules (a rough guess with a wide range). Together that is about 390 billion kilowatt-hours: about five days of the world's electricity, between half a day and about seven weeks if a flash holds ten times less or more.

Series: Season 2, episode 6 of 6.

Sources
1. Global flash rate about 44 flashes a second (Christian et al. 2003, from NASA's Optical Transient Detector). — DOI: https://doi.org/10.1029/2002JD002347
2. 44 x 31.6 million s = 1.39 billion flashes a year (calc.py: flashes). — Derivation (no outside page; arithmetic shown in the film)
3. Energy of one lightning flash is of order 1e9 J (a billion joules), with a wide range (about 1e8 to 1e10 J). — weather.gov: https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-overview
4. 1.39e9 flashes x 1e9 J = 1.39e18 J = 386 billion kWh (1 kWh = 3.6e6 J). — Derivation (no outside page; arithmetic shown in the film)
5. World electricity generation is about 30,000 TWh a year (about 2023); 30,000 / 386 = 78. — ember-energy.org: https://ember-energy.org/data/electricity-data-explorer/
6. 386 TWh / 30,000 TWh = 1.3% of a year = 4.7 days (calc.py: days). — Derivation (no outside page; arithmetic shown in the film)
7. At 1e8 J a flash: 0.5 days; at 1e10 J: 47 days (6.7 weeks). — Derivation (no outside page; arithmetic shown in the film)
8. Most of a lightning flash's energy is released as heat, light, sound and radio waves, and a flash lasts a fraction of a second. — weather.gov: https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-overview
```
