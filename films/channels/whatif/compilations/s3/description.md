What If Earth Spun Faster and Couldn't Stop?

Shorten the day to about 1 hour 24 minutes and the ground at the equator moves at orbital speed: you weigh nothing. We follow one person standing on the equator as the day falls from 24 hours down to 1.4: the Sun races, the air is left behind, the oceans slide toward the equator, storms tighten, the year fills with thousands of days, you get lighter, and then the planet cannot hold on. The last minutes cover the float, where the push outward cancels gravity, the point where the equator breaks up, and what it would cost in energy.

Sources (every number is a first-order estimate for a perfect ball; each claim is still OPEN until a person opens its source)
- The Earth turns once in 23 hours 56 minutes 4 seconds; its radius is 6,378 km at the equator and 6,357 km at the poles. (NASA Goddard, Earth Fact Sheet): https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html
- The ground at the equator moves at about 1,674 km/h (465 m/s): one trip around the equator divided by the length of a day. That is about 1/17 of the speed needed to orbit. (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- The spin makes you about 0.35% lighter today: the outward push of the spin is 0.034 m/s², against gravity's 9.8 m/s². (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- Speed of the ground at the equator: 1,674 km/h today, 3,340 km/h at a 12 h day, 6,679 km/h at 6 h, 13,358 km/h at 3 h, 20,038 km/h at 2 h and 26,717 km/h at 1.5 h. The Sun is up for half of each day. (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- Your weight as a share of what it would be on a non-spinning Earth: 99.7% today, 98.6% at a 12 h day, 94.5% at 6 h, 78.0% at 3 h, 50.4% at 2 h and 11.9% at 1.5 h. A 70 kg person weighs 69 kg at 12 h and 55 kg at 3 h. (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- The faster Earth spins, the higher the sea wants to stand at the equator compared with the poles: 33 km higher at a 12 h day, and far more as the day gets shorter. (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- If the air kept turning at today's rate, the wind relative to the ground would be 463 m/s (1,667 km/h) at a 12 h day and 1,390 m/s at a 6 h day, until friction drags the air along. (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- At a day of about 84.5 minutes (1.41 h) the spin cancels gravity at the equator: the ground moves at 7.9 km/s, and today's 0.465 km/s is 1/17 of that. (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- The spin energy of the Earth is about 2.1 × 10²⁹ J today and about 6.2 × 10³¹ J at a 1.41 h day, 289 times more. That is what the Sun delivers to the Earth in about 11 million years. (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- Past the speed where the outward push equals gravity, loose material at the equator is no longer held; how a real planet would break up is an assumption, not a calculation. (Assumption (hedge): a thought experiment, not a model of the Earth breaking up)
- The sunlight reaching the Earth is about 1,361 watts per square meter (the solar constant). (Kopp and Lean, A new, lower value of total solar irradiance, Geophysical Research Letters 38 (2011)): https://doi.org/10.1029/2010GL045777
- Today the Earth already bulges about 21 km wider at the equator than pole to pole (6,378 km against 6,357 km). (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- We use a 70 kg person as an example, not a measurement; every figure is a first-order estimate for a perfect ball. (Assumption: an illustrative 70 kg person)
- The speed of sound in air at sea level is about 340 m/s. The equator moves at 2.7 times that at a 12 h day (929 m/s) and 1.35 times at 6 h (463 m/s). (NASA Glenn, Speed of sound): https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/sound.html
- Storm steering (the Coriolis effect) grows in direct proportion to the spin rate: 4 times today's at a 6 h day and about 17 times at a 1.4 h day. (Our calculation (shown in the film))
- At a day of 1 hour 24 minutes a year holds about 6,200 days (365.25 days × 23.93 h ÷ 1.41 h). (Our calculation (shown in the film))
