## opening {shot: card} (__)
vo: Six questions. Each one sounds impossible, and each has an answer you can work out on the back of an envelope. One simple calculation, again and again, beats gut feeling. Let's open the first envelope.

## bridge1 {shot: card} (__)
vo: Twenty-six years to walk to the Moon, a working day at a time. Now imagine everyone on Earth holding hands. How far would that chain reach?

## bridge2 {shot: card} (__)
vo: A chain that wraps the Earth about three hundred times. Let's go back to a single loop around the planet, and one rope.

## bridge3 {shot: card} (__)
vo: Lift that rope one meter, and you need only about six meters more, on a planet of any size. Now for a number almost too big to say: a shuffled deck.

## bridge4 {shot: card} (__)
vo: Sixty-eight digits, from a single deck of cards. Now something huge in size instead: all the water on Earth, gathered into one ball.

## bridge5 {shot: card} (__)
vo: A ball of water fourteen hundred kilometers across. Now something fast and wild: could we catch all the lightning on Earth?

## finale {shot: card} (__)
vo: Six envelopes. Here they are, from the smallest surprise to the biggest. Walking to the Moon: twenty-six years, long, but easy to believe. A whole year of lightning: roughly five days of the world's electricity. All the water in one ball: fourteen hundred kilometers. Everyone holding hands: three hundred laps around the planet. A shuffled deck: an answer with sixty-eight digits. And the rope: just six more meters, whatever the size of the planet. Wait, what?
