# What a person must check before this posts

## This episode
- **The physics** (c1, c3, c4, c7): momentum conservation for the isolated Earth-plus-people system; the shared centre
  of mass doesn't change course, so the orbit is untouched; the Earth's body moves by N·m·h/M and returns on landing.
  A physicist should sign off the wording "drops away beneath our feet" and "settles right back where it was".
- **The thought experiment's assumption:** everyone in one place (badged). Spread round the real globe, the pushes
  point every which way and largely cancel; the film says so in claims.json, not aloud.
- **Inputs:** 8.2 billion (UN 2024); 62 kg is the world's average *adult* mass (Walpole 2012), so it overstates
  children (an upper bound); 40 cm is a mid value of adult standing-jump norms (about 30–50 cm; the Topend Sports
  norms and a 116-person study are secondary sources: a sports scientist may prefer a peer-reviewed norm).
- **"Three thousand times smaller than a single atom"** compares with hydrogen (2 Bohr radii, 0.106 nm), the smallest
  atom: the conservative choice. The ladder's hair (≈ 0.1 mm) and red blood cell (≈ 7 µm) are scale markers from
  Wikipedia's "Micrometre" (ranges 17–181 µm and 6–8 µm).
- **The model Earth** is drawn not to scale, and the dip and jump are exaggerated (badged). The ghost globe "knocked
  off course?" is the wrong idea, shown and withdrawn.
- **AI labelling:** "Narration: synthetic voice (AI)" on the post, and the platform's AI-content label.
- **Prior art:** xkcd's What If? covered "everybody jump"; this film shares no jokes, art or lines, and its sum,
  sources and atom comparison are its own (bible §9).
- **Pronunciation at T0:** "ten trillion", "atom".
