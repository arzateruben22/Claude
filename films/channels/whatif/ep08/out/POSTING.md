**Description**

```
How long is a chain of us? Everyone on Earth, holding hands.

Stretch your arms out and you are about as wide as you are tall. Give everyone a meter and a half, kids included, and 8.2 billion of us joined hand to hand make a line about 12 million kilometers long. Wrapped round the Earth, that is about 310 laps, and one lap needs only about one person in 300 of us. The same line would reach the Moon about 32 times.

Series: Season 2, episode 2 of 6.

Sources
1. An adult's arm span (fingertip to fingertip) is about equal to body height (ratio about 1.0; roughly 1.03 in men and 1.01 in women in some studies). — pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32575104/
2. Assumption, not a measurement: average arm span, kids included, about 1.5 m per person: adults about 1.6 to 1.7 m mixed with roughly a quarter to 30% of people under 18, who are shorter. Shown as a green '∵ 1.5 m a person' badge; sensitivity 1.4 m and 1.7 m (adults only). — doi.org: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.13410
3. World population about 8.2 billion (mid-2025); the film says 'eight billion' (rounded down) and uses 8.2 billion in the sum. — population.un.org: https://population.un.org/wpp/
4. 8.2e9 people x 1.5 m = 12.3 million km: 'about twelve million kilometers'. — population.un.org: https://population.un.org/wpp/
5. 12.3 million km / 40,075 km (equator) = 307: 'about three hundred and ten times'. — NASA: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/

7. At 1.4 m a person: about 290 laps; at 1.7 m: about 350 laps (shown on the work card), against 310 at 1.5 m. — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html
8. 12.3 million km / 384,400 km (the Moon's mean distance) = 32 one-way trips (shown '≈ 32 trips to the Moon'). — NASA: https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/
```
