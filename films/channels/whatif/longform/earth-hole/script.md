# Possibility Lab · Season 3 · Episode 2: "What If You Fell Into a Hole Through the Earth?" (script v1; fact-check before any render)

About 9 min, 16:9 (2560x1440), for YouTube's main feed, narrated by a synthetic voice for now (the owner will record it), with an original score. A thought experiment:
a tube runs straight through the centre of the Earth, from pole to pole, with no air and no friction. You step in and let go. Every number comes from `calc.py`
(arithmetic on the Earth's size and gravity; the real, denser Earth is carried as OPEN sources); `claims.json` ties each sentence to a source or marks it a derivation.

The picture: you, falling down a lit shaft; ONE big live number is always on screen, your depth (km), with your speed under it; it escalates continuously and never
resets: depth climbs to 6,371 km at the far side while the speed rises, peaks at the centre, and falls again. Each milestone is a beat where something visibly changes
(the wall of the shaft: crust, mantle, liquid core, solid core). The ending is "wait, what?": any straight tunnel, even one that misses the centre, takes the same 42 minutes.
House rules: one text element per idea; the caption carries the sentence; no voiceless gap over 1.2 s; motion every 2 to 3 s; badge "Thought experiment" throughout.

One line per beat. `*starred*` words get a camera landing or a sound hit. `{c1,c2}` after a sentence ties it to `claims.json`; `{-}` marks a sentence that states nothing outside the film.

## hook {shot: you at the rim of a round shaft in the ground at dawn; the day counter shows depth 0 km; you step off · badge: thought experiment · title: "What If You Fell Into a Hole Through the Earth?" · energy: 0.6} (__)
vo: What if you could dig a hole straight through the middle of the Earth, all the way to the other side, and jump in? {-} It would take you forty-two minutes to come out the other end. {c3} And on the way, you would pass through some of the strangest places on the planet. {-}

## rules {shot: a cutaway of the Earth with a thin tube from pole to pole; the labels light one by one · energy: 0.5} (__)
vo: Here are the rules. {-} The tube runs from pole to pole, straight through the centre. {-} There is no air in it, and nothing rubs against you. {-} You do not need to dig it, cool it or hold it up. {-} You simply stand at the top, and you let go. {-} Later, we will see why none of this could ever be built. {-}

## numbers {shot: the depth readout appears and the speed under it; both at zero · energy: 0.45} (__)
vo: On screen there are two numbers: how deep you are, and how fast you are falling. {-} Depth starts at zero, and by the end it will have reached six thousand, three hundred and seventy kilometers. {c1} Speed starts at zero too, and it will not stop rising until the middle. {-} Nothing resets. {-}

## first {shot: the shaft lights run past; the counters climb; the sky above shrinks to a coin · milestone: the first minute · energy: 0.6} (__)
vo: In the first second, you fall about five meters, just like any falling object. {c4} After a minute, you are seventeen kilometers down, falling at almost six hundred meters a second, faster than the speed of sound. {c4} {c5} You are already deeper than any hole humans have ever drilled. {c6}

## crust {shot: the shaft wall is rock: layers of grey and red slide past; a temperature readout beside the depth · milestone: the crust · energy: 0.65} (__)
vo: At about thirty-five kilometers, you leave the crust, the thin skin of rock we live on. {c7} It has taken you less than a minute and a half. {c4} Down here, the rock around the tube is already hot enough to glow, because the Earth gets warmer by about twenty-five degrees for every kilometer you descend. {c8} But the tube is made of imagination, so you keep falling. {-}

## mantle {shot: the wall turns orange and slow-moving; the speed readout is now in kilometers a second; the pull is weaker · milestone: the mantle · energy: 0.7} (__)
vo: Next comes the mantle, nearly three thousand kilometers of hot rock that flows, very slowly, like thick tar. {c7} Its temperature climbs from about a thousand degrees to nearly four thousand. {c9} You are now moving at over five kilometers a second. {c4} And something odd is happening. {-} You are still speeding up, but more and more gently, because the pull of gravity is getting weaker as you go down. {c10}

## shell {shot: a cutaway: a shell of rock above you and a ball below; arrows show the pull from the ball only; the pull readout falls · milestone: why gravity weakens · energy: 0.6} (__)
vo: Why does the pull get weaker as you go down? {-} Because of a lovely rule that Isaac Newton worked out. {c24} Inside a ball, all the rock above you pulls in every direction at once, and its pull cancels out completely. {c24} Only the rock below you still pulls you down. {c24} The deeper you go, the less rock is below you, so the pull shrinks, steadily, until at the very centre there is nothing below you at all. {c10} {c24}

## outer {shot: the wall is liquid metal: bright, churning; you are weightless-ish; speed 6.6 km/s · milestone: the outer core · energy: 0.8} (__)
vo: Thirteen minutes in, you cross into the outer core. {c4} It is a sea of liquid iron and nickel, as hot as the surface of the Sun. {c9} {c11} Nothing solid is left. {-} If this tube were not imaginary, you would not survive another second. {-} But you do, and you are falling at about six and a half kilometers a second, nearly twenty-four thousand kilometers an hour. {c4} {c12}

## inner {shot: the wall is a bright solid ball: the inner core; the pull is almost gone; speed 7.8 km/s · milestone: the inner core · energy: 0.85} (__)
vo: Eighteen and a half minutes in, you reach the inner core, a solid ball of iron about as wide as the dwarf planet Pluto. {c4} {c13} It is solid because the pressure down here is so enormous that it squeezes the iron into a solid, even though it is as hot as the Sun. {c14} Your speed is almost at its peak, and gravity, the force that has been pulling you, is almost gone. {c4} {c10}

## centre {shot: the centre of the Earth: a glowing point; weightless; the speed readout shows its peak; the depth readout shows the half-way mark · milestone: the centre · energy: 0.95} (__)
vo: Twenty-one minutes after you let go, you pass the exact centre of the Earth. {c3} Here, the pull of gravity is zero. {c10} You are weightless, and you are moving at your highest speed: about seven point nine kilometers a second, nearly twenty-eight and a half thousand kilometers an hour. {c3} {c15} That is fast enough to cross the United States, coast to coast, in under ten minutes. {c16} And you carry on, because you cannot stop. {-}

## second {shot: the wall slides past in reverse order: inner core, outer core, mantle, crust; the speed falls; the depth readout falls · milestone: the climb back up · energy: 0.7} (__)
vo: Now gravity pulls you back. {-} Everything you passed on the way down, you pass again in reverse, and you slow down by exactly the same amounts. {c10} The depth number starts to fall, and so does the speed. {-} The trip is a perfect mirror image of the first half. {-}

## far {shot: the far end of the tube: the sky ring grows; the speed readout reaches zero; you float at the rim for an instant · milestone: the far side · energy: 0.8} (__)
vo: Forty-two minutes after you started, you reach the far end of the tube, and your speed falls to exactly zero. {c3} You have gone all the way through the Earth, and you have just enough speed to reach the other opening and no more. {c10} For an instant, you hang there. {-} If a friend does not catch you, you start to fall back in. {-}

## swing {shot: a clock beside the tube counts the swings; a trace shows the position swinging up and down · milestone: the swing never stops · energy: 0.7} (__)
vo: With no air and no friction, you would never stop. {-} You would swing from one end of the Earth to the other and back again, over and over, once every eighty-four minutes. {c3} That is about seventeen times a day, for ever. {c17} {c3}

## real {shot: a second line on the graph, steeper: the real Earth, with a denser core · milestone: the honest correction · energy: 0.6} (__)
vo: One honest correction. {-} This is for an Earth that is the same density all the way through. {-} The real Earth is far denser at the middle, so gravity stays stronger for longer, and you would fall a little faster. {c18} The real trip takes about thirty-eight minutes, not forty-two, and you would pass the centre a bit quicker than seven point nine. {c18} The shape of the trip stays the same. {-}

## wait {shot: three tunnels across the Earth: a straight one through the middle, a shorter chord, a shallow chord; three markers start together · milestone: wait, what? · energy: 0.9} (__)
vo: Now, here is the strangest part. {-} Suppose the tunnel does not go through the centre. {-} Suppose it is a shortcut between two cities, a straight tunnel that never gets near the middle. {-} How long would that take? {-} Forty-two minutes. {c19} The same. {c19} A short tunnel, a long tunnel, or one that goes through the centre. {-} They all take forty-two minutes, with no engine and no fuel. {c19}

## why {shot: the tube: heat, pressure, air, the spinning planet; each as a short beat · milestone: the real world · energy: 0.6} (__)
vo: In the real world, none of this works, for good reasons. {-} The deepest hole ever drilled, in Russia, only reached twelve kilometers, and the rock there was already over one hundred and eighty degrees. {c6} {c20} Down at the centre, the pressure is about three point six million times the air pressure you feel now. {c14} And if the tube had air in it, the air would slow you down to a few dozen meters a second, no faster than a skydiver. {c21} Even the spinning Earth would get in the way: you would be thrown sideways into the wall, except on a path from pole to pole. {c22} {-}

## close {shot: the surface at dawn; you step back from the rim; the readouts at zero · energy: 0.4} (__)
vo: So you cannot jump through the Earth. {-} But the maths is lovely. {-} That swing through the middle takes exactly as long as it would take to orbit the Earth, a little under an hour and a half. {c3} {c23} It is the same rhythm, in two different places. {-} Thanks for watching. {-}
