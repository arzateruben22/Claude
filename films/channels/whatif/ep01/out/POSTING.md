How long to fall through the Earth? Step in, and let go.

That whole trip, the textbooks said, takes forty-two minutes. But they pictured the same stuff all the way down. A graduate student redid the sum with the real Earth, and its heavy core makes gravity grow stronger as you fall. So a fall through the Earth is more than a ride. It's a way of weighing its heart.

Sources
1. A tunnel through Earth's centre is one diameter long: 2 × 6,371 km = 12,742 km, said as about 12,700 km. — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html
2. Falling from rest at the surface with no air, the speed at Earth's centre is about 10 km/s (PREM density: 9.92 km/s; a uniform Earth gives 7.91 km/s). — doi.org: https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-9201(81)90046-7
3. In the idealised tunnel (no air or friction, no rotation) the faller decelerates past the centre and comes to rest exactly at the far surface: energy is conserved and the potential is symmetric. — arxiv.org: https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.1342
4. The classic textbook answer for a fall through a uniform-density Earth is about 42 minutes (42 min 12 s), from P. W. Cooper, Am. J. Phys. 34, 68 (1966). — livescience.com: https://www.livescience.com/50312-how-long-to-fall-through-earth.html
