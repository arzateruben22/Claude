The sunscreen experiment | Skin Sense · Episode 5

**Description**

```
Which hand aged? After four and a half years, one group showed no extra ageing.

It is easy to think sun damage is the burn you feel. But an ultraviolet camera shows damage your eyes miss, long before it appears on the surface. So, which hand aged? Both groups missed some mornings. One missed fewer, and the skin kept count.

Series: Season 1: The organ you live inside, part 5 of 6. Next: Same list as cigarettes?

Educational only, not medical advice. This video names no products and gives no dosing or treatment advice; talk to a clinician about your own skin.

Sources
1. After 4.5 years, the daily sunscreen group showed no detectable increase in skin ageing; the relative odds of increased ageing in the daily vs discretionary group were 0.76 (95% CI 0.59 to 0.98), i.e. 24% less likely. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23732711/
2. The Nambour Skin Cancer Prevention Trial (Queensland, Australia) randomly assigned 1,621 randomly selected adult residents of Nambour to daily or discretionary sunscreen use; the trial ran from 1992 to 1996 (February 1992 to August 1996 in one report). — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21135266/
3. UV photography shows sun damage that cannot be seen with the naked eye; extensive damage beneath the surface can build up long before signs show on the skin. — American Academy of Dermatology: https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/sun-protection/sun-damage-skin/hidden-sun-damage
4. Melanoma is the most serious (deadliest) form of skin cancer. — newswise.com: https://www.newswise.com/articles/american-academy-of-dermatology-releases-guidelines-for-melanoma-treatment
5. SPF testing applies sunscreen at 2 mg/cm²; at a density of about 1 g/cm³ that is a film about 20 µm thick (16–17 µm for denser products), about a fifth of a ~100 µm sheet of paper (computed). — tga.gov.au: https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/consult-otc-argom-sunscreens-100505-submission-antaria.pdf
6. In the Nambour trial, the daily group applied sunscreen more often than the discretionary group, though neither group used it every day. — mdedge.com: https://mdedge.com/familymedicine/article/59024/dermatology/daily-sunscreen-slowed-skin-aging-middle-aged-adults
7. IARC (the WHO's cancer agency) classifies UV-emitting tanning devices in Group 1, 'carcinogenic to humans', the same group as tobacco smoking (2009). — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19655431/
```
