**Description**

Feel that? A shiver.

Your brain answers in two soft steps. vo: First, the little blood vessels near your skin narrow. Warm blood stays deeper inside you. One cool breeze. Skin, nerves, vessels and muscle, all answering together. All… without asking you.

Series: Season 3: Inside your skin, slowly, part 1 of 6.

Sources
1. Cold-sensitive endings in the skin signal through sensory neurons toward the brain's temperature-control centre (the hypothalamus). — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3098654
2. The brain answers cold with several cold-defence responses, in order of rising energy cost: vessel narrowing in the skin first, shivering last (goosebumps also occur). The film simplifies to two steps plus goosebumps. — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4846468
3. Shivering raises heat production several-fold over the resting rate (peak about five times resting reported; moderate shivering about 3.5 times). — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11394237/
4. Sympathetic nerve signals make the arrector pili muscle beside each hair follicle contract, pulling the hair upright (piloerection, goosebumps). — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32679029/
