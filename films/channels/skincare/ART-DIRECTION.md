# Skin Sense: Approved Art Direction

Use the accompanying Episode 4 and going forward image as the visual reference
for future Skin Sense artwork.
(Reference file: `films/channels/skincare/reference/ep04-frown-reference.webp`, "The Message Behind a Frown".)

## Visual style
Premium medical illustration with realistic tissue textures:
translucent membranes, fine fibres, delicate branching structures,
soft lighting, subtle moisture, and convincing depth.

Use warm ivory backgrounds, deep navy typography, peach and
rose tissue, pale gold nerves, and restrained teal molecules.
Keep the overall appearance calm, detailed, and elegant.

## Composition
- Vertical videos: 1080 × 1920.
- Large editorial serif headline above the main illustration.
- Clean sans-serif supporting text.
- One clear anatomical subject per shot.
- Small ivory label pills with navy text and thin leader lines.
- Soft transitions between tissue artwork and ivory background.
- Comfortable margins and readable text on a phone.
- Skin Sense branding throughout.

## Production
Generate artwork separately from typography where possible.
Add crisp, editable titles and labels during video composition.

Use restrained camera movement and purposeful animation tied
to narration. Keep labels attached to their structures.
A zoom on a still is a camera move, not anatomical animation.

Preserve the approved visual language across scenes while
changing the anatomy and composition to fit each explanation.

## Scientific accuracy
Treat the reference as an art-direction example, not an
anatomical authority. Check structures, labels, and mechanisms
against reliable sources. Identify conceptual simplifications.

## Avoid
Flat cartoon diagrams, plastic-looking tissue, excessive glow,
busy layouts, unreadable captions, gore, and copied creator branding.

## Immediate scope
Start with one short Episode 4 sample. Reuse approved assets
where appropriate and keep production costs low. Review the
sample before producing the full episode or additional episodes.
