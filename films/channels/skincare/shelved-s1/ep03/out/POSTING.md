Bath fingers | Outer Layer No. 003

**Description**

```
Why do wet fingers wrinkle? Your arm stays smooth in the same bath.

So the wrinkles come from your nerves. In water, they narrow the tiny vessels under each fingertip, the pad shrinks a little, and the skin on top buckles. So your fingers don't wrinkle because they're soaked. They wrinkle because your nerves decide to.

Series: Season 1: The organ you live inside, part 3 of 6.

Sources
1. Water-immersion wrinkling occurs in the glabrous (hairless) skin of the fingers, palms and soles, not in hairy skin such as the forearm. — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3885627/
2. Water-immersion wrinkling is commonly attributed to skin swelling by osmosis (absorbing water); that explanation is not supported. — nutrition-evidence.com: https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/58149/water-immersion-wrinkling-physiology-and-use-as-an-indicator-of-sympathetic-function
3. A 2013 study found wrinkled fingers moved submerged objects faster than unwrinkled ones (no effect for dry objects). — eprints.ncl.ac.uk: https://eprints.ncl.ac.uk/189064
```
