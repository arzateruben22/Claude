What a tan is | Outer Layer No. 004

**Description**

```
Is a tan a shield? A thin one, built after the harm.

In Boston, mice whose skin cells lacked that guardian never tanned at all. So a tan isn't skin getting ready for the sun. It's skin answering damage already done. So, is a tan a shield? A thin one. It's how your skin remembers the light.

Series: Season 1: The organ you live inside, part 4 of 6.

Sources
1. A tan gives only modest protection: about the equivalent of SPF 3–4. — health.harvard.edu: https://www.health.harvard.edu/skin-and-hair/are-there-benefits-to-a-base-tan
2. A tan develops in response to UV damage to skin cells: it follows the injury, it does not precede it. — aspe.hhs.gov: https://aspe.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/private/pdf/261976/67b_Response_to_RFC.pdf
3. The shorter-wavelength ultraviolet (UVB) is absorbed mostly in the epidermis; its DNA damage is found mainly in the living keratinocytes above the basal layer (UVA reaches deeper). — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC387355
4. UV light makes cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers (two adjacent DNA bases, thymines, bonded together) within about 1 picosecond (a trillionth of a second) of absorption. — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2792699
5. Skin cells detect UV-induced DNA damage: the damage activates the p53 protein. — sciencedaily.com: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/03/070308122006.htm
6. Delayed tanning (new melanin, driven mostly by UVB) becomes visible about 48–72 hours after exposure. — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2593890
7. Keratinocytes take up melanin from melanocytes and arrange it above the nucleus as a supranuclear cap, which shields the DNA from UV by absorbing and scattering it. — nms.unl.pt: https://www.nms.unl.pt/en-us/research/science-and-society/article-at-a-glance/202307-duarte-barral-en-us
8. A keratinocyte nucleus is about 8.6 µm across (estimate from reflectance confocal microscopy; whole cells 15–35 µm); the melanin cap covers the nucleus, so it is about that wide. — doi.org: https://doi.org/10.1117/1.3552639
9. A sheet of ordinary 80 gsm copy paper is about 100 µm thick. — resource.download.wjec.co.uk: https://resource.download.wjec.co.uk/vtc/2016-17/16-17_1-4/website/category/5/1papers_and_boards/category_and_standards/index.html
```
