**The story (Flow standard):** bigger number, bigger shield? Not the way you'd think. SPF is a ratio, how much more
burning light the skin takes under the film before it reddens; flipped, both let only a sliver through, so the bigger
number blocks barely more. But look at what gets in: half as much, at the lab's thickness. Real people spread a
quarter to half as much, and protection falls well below the label (how far is argued). The lab layer is about as thick
as the dead layer from No. 001, a fifth of a sheet of paper. So the bigger number isn't twice the shield; it's half the
light getting in, when there's enough of it.
