**Description**

```
Bigger number, bigger shield? On a sunscreen, not the way you'd think.

But look at what actually gets in. Half as much. That's the real difference, as long as the film is as thick as the lab's. So the bigger number isn't twice the shield. It's half the light getting in, when there's enough of it.

Series: Season 1: The organ you live inside, part 2 of 6.

Sources
1. A sunscreen's SPF is not a percentage of protection; doubling SPF does not double the share of UVB blocked. — federal-regs.com: https://federal-regs.com/title/21/part-352/352.3/
2. Under the idealised reading of SPF, a product transmits about 1/SPF of sunburning UV: SPF 50 about 2% (≈98% blocked), SPF 100 about 1% (≈99% blocked). — American Academy of Dermatology: https://aad.org/news/aad-survey-shows-americans-need-sunscreen-education
3. Studies of real-world use find people apply about 0.39–1.0 mg/cm², roughly a fifth to a half of the 2 mg/cm² test amount. — doi.org: https://doi.org/10.1111/phpp.12099
```
