**The story (Flow standard):** why do we get goosebumps when we hardly have fur? Nearly every hair has a tiny muscle;
cold or fear makes a nerve tug it, standing the hair and raising a bump. In a furry animal that fluffs the coat into a
blanket of trapped air; we have about as many follicles as a chimpanzee, but ours grow fine hairs, so the bumps look like
a leftover. A Harvard team (Shwartz et al., Cell, 2020, in mice) found the same nerve reaches the hair's stem cells and
the muscle holds it in place: in the cold the nerve wakes them and new hair grows sooner; take the muscle away and the
wiring fades. Most of our hairs are thinner than a third of a sheet of paper. We still get goosebumps because the muscle
that raises a hair still looks after it.
