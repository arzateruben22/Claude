# The sixth topic: options and pick

The other five are pimples, skin glow, Botox, sunscreen after ten years and tanning beds. The sixth should not
lean on UV (two of the five already do) and should give the season one topic about repair. Freckles (MC1R,
sun-triggered, fading in winter) was dropped early: it is another UV-and-pigment story after tanning beds and
the original No. 004/005. All numbers below were checked through search excerpts only (page fetches are blocked).

## Option A: What's in a scar

- **Hook:** "Is a scar still *skin*? Look closer, thread by thread."
- **Angle:** the body doesn't rebuild skin; it patches it. Normal dermis is a basket weave of collagen; a scar is
  parallel bundles with no hair or sweat glands, and it never gets its full strength back. The whoa: before about
  24 weeks of gestation, we heal without scars at all.
- **Three numbers:**
  1. Healed skin reaches only **about 80%** of the tensile strength of unwounded skin (Levenson et al. 1965, rat
     skin; via Ireton, Unger & Rohrich 2013, PRS Global Open, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4174176/).
  2. Human fetal skin heals without scars until **about 22–24 weeks** of gestation (review of fetal scarless
     healing 2012, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3362931/; Larson, Longaker & Lorenz 2010, PRS
     126:1172–80, "approximately 24 weeks").
  3. Only the epidermis regenerates fully, and on most of the body it is **about 0.07–0.12 mm**, a paper's
     thickness (Int J Mol Sci 2021;22:897, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7831008/; Atlas of
     Microscopic Anatomy §7, https://anatomyatlases.org/MicroscopicAnatomy/Section07/Section07.shtml).
- **Why it stops a scroll:** everyone has a scar they can look at right now, and two facts land hard together:
  that "healed" skin is a permanent four-fifths-strength patch, and that you once healed without one. No product,
  no advice, no UV: pure biology with a clean picture (weave → parallel threads).

## Option B: Why we wrinkle

- **Hook:** "Which half of this man's face is older?" (shot idea: the trucker photo described as a `model`
  diagram, never the real photo without rights).
- **Angle:** wrinkles are mostly not time but light. A delivery driver's left side, by the window for decades, is
  far more wrinkled than his right; the collagen and elastin under it are what UVA wrecked.
- **Three numbers:**
  1. A driver exposed through the side window for **about 28 years** (Northwestern says "more than 25") showed
     one-sided photoageing (Gordon & Brieva, "Unilateral dermatoheliosis", NEJM 2012;366:e25).
  2. UV exposure "seems to be responsible for **80%** of visible facial aging signs" in 298 French women aged
     30–78 (Flament et al. 2013, Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3790843/;
     observational, industry-authored).
  3. Skin collagen is often said to fall **about 1% a year** in adulthood (Shuster, Black & McVitie 1975, Br J
     Dermatol 93:639–43), but no excerpt confirms that the paper states 1%; postmenopausal women lose **1–2% a
     year** (Brincat et al. 1987, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3120067/). Unverified as phrased.
- **Why it stops a scroll:** the half-and-half face is a famous image, and "most of what you call ageing is light"
  reframes the mirror. **Against it:** it overlaps sunscreen-after-ten-years and tanning beds (third UV episode
  of six), the trucker image needs rights or a redraw, the 80% is one industry-funded study, and the 1% figure
  does not survive sourcing.

## Pick: Option A, "What's in a scar"

It has the strongest number that survives checking (about 80%, from a named classic study), a genuine whoa
(scarless before about 24 weeks) and a built-in "to scale" (the regrowing layer is about a paper's thickness).
It also gives the season a topic that isn't UV, a product or a treatment, so it sits furthest from advice.
