**Title**

Where Do Your Skin Cells Go?

**Description**

```
Deep in your skin, right now, a new cell is being born.

Near the top, it lets go of its nucleus… and its life gently ends. Then, quietly, the top cells drift away, far too small to see. Some of the dust in your home… began as you.

Series: Season 3: Inside your skin, slowly, part 2 of 6.

Sources
1. New skin cells are produced continuously in the deepest layer of the epidermis (the basal layer, where stem cells divide). — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK513299
2. Corneocytes embedded in lipids form a barrier that limits water loss (the bricks-and-mortar model). — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7592940
3. Epidermal turnover takes roughly a month (about 28 days or more, varying with age and site). — bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu: https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=109215
4. Skin scales (desquamated skin) are a real component of the particles in room air and dust; the share varies widely, and 'most dust is skin' is not supported. — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2130262
```
