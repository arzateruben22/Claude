Where a glow comes from | Skin Sense No. 002

**Description**

```
Where's your glow from? Most light on your face goes inside you.

You'd picture a glow as shine sitting on the skin. But it's light that has travelled through you, past your melanin and your blood. Film faces made by computer once looked like plastic: their light never went inside. Yours does; that's your glow.

Series: Season 1: Under the surface, part 2 of 6. Next: is a scar still skin?

Sources
1. Since only about 4–7% is reflected at the surface, the great majority of light striking skin (about 93–96%) enters it. — doi.org: https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JBO.17.9.090901
2. Hydrating the stratum corneum makes skin measurably smoother (and the stratum corneum thicker), with the fastest water uptake in the upper few micrometres. — doi.org: https://doi.org/10.1116/1.4962547
3. A smoother surface reflects light more specularly (in one direction, mirror-like); skin's perceived gloss falls as its microscale roughness rises. — library.imaging.org: https://library.imaging.org/jpi/articles/8/0/000502
4. When skin is dehydrated, its surface (stratum corneum) scatters more light; hydration lowers that scattering and makes skin more translucent. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20923460/
5. Light inside skin is scattered many times by cells and fibres; in one worked example about 40% of incident light is backscattered out of the skin after multiple scattering, against about 5% surface glare (the rest, about 53%, absorbed). — omlc.org: https://omlc.org/news/oct99/saratov/page3.htm
6. Blood flow makes the face's colour (its redness) vary slightly at the heart rate; the variation is difficult or impossible to see with the naked eye, but video processing can amplify and reveal it. — dspace.mit.edu: https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/77452
7. Plethysmographic (pulse) signals, including heart rate, were measured remotely (more than 1 m away) from the face using ambient light and a simple consumer-level digital camera in movie mode; the green channel gave the strongest signal, matching oxyhaemoglobin's absorption. — doi.org: https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.16.021434
8. In skin, blue light barely penetrates about 1 mm, whereas red light is extinguished some 4–5 mm beneath the surface; penetration increases with wavelength. — doi.org: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-017-2317-4
9. A sheet of ordinary 80 gsm copy paper is about 100 µm thick. — resource.download.wjec.co.uk: https://resource.download.wjec.co.uk/vtc/2016-17/16-17_1-4/website/category/5/1papers_and_boards/category_and_standards/index.html
10. Red light (about 625–700 nm) can reach through the full thickness of the skin, while blue light stays near the surface. — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8887026/
11. Earlier computer-graphics shading assumed light reflects from the point where it hits a surface, which made skin look hard, like plastic; a 2001 model of subsurface light transport (light entering a translucent material and leaving elsewhere) gave skin its soft look and earned its authors a 2004 Academy Technical Achievement Award for its use in films. — graphics.stanford.edu: https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/bssrdf/
12. Early in gestation (until about 22–24 weeks in humans) fetal skin wounds heal without a scar (the tease for No. 003; the same source as No. 003's c11). — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3362931/
```
