## what {shot S02: the panel's three LED colours light in turn, with labels 465, 640 and 880 nm; the last is drawn as dots, "invisible" · energy: 0.45}
vo: The Celluma PRO uses three wavelengths: blue at four hundred and sixty-five nanometres, red at six hundred and forty, and invisible near-infrared at eight hundred and eighty. {c1} Blue aims at acne bacteria in the pore. Red and near-infrared aim at the cells beneath. {-}

## programs {shot S03: three program chips (Acne, Wrinkles, Aches & Pains) and a 30-minute timer ring · energy: 0.45}

## fda {shot S04: a clean card: "FDA 510(k) clearances K152280, K171323", then three icons appear: a pore, a fine line, a joint · energy: 0.5}
vo: Its FDA clearances cover blue light for mild to moderate inflammatory acne, {c3} visible and infrared light for full-face wrinkles, {c4} and infrared heat for temporary relief of minor muscle and joint pain. {c3} Cleared means the FDA found it substantially equivalent to devices already sold. It isn't the same as approved. {c5}

## fit {shot S05: the panel curves around the faceless head and neck, then a forearm, then the upper back, hands-free; a small "1 inch" gap marker · energy: 0.45}
vo: The panel is flexible. It curves around the face and neck, an arm or the back, without being held, about an inch or less from bare skin. {c6} That matters, because results depend on how much light actually reaches the skin. {c7}
