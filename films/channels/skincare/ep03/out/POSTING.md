**Title**

What's in a scar | Skin Sense No. 003

**Description**

```
Why do you scar now? Before birth, a cut healed without one.

We call that healed, as good as new. But pulled until it tears, even an old scar is only as strong as a rope missing one strand in five. So, why the scar? Mice born without germ-fighting cells heal without one: a scar may be the price of defence.

Series: Season 1: Under the surface, part 3 of 6. Next: a deadly poison, in a frown. How much goes in?

Sources
1. The dermis's strength comes from collagen fibre bundles arranged in a criss-cross, basket-weave network, which lets normal skin give in many directions. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18797087/
2. When an injury reaches the dermis, adult skin repairs it with scar tissue rather than regenerating the original dermis. — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7831008/
3. Ordinary scars lack hair follicles and sweat glands (dermal appendages). — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6485243/
4. Healed skin wounds reach only about 80% of the tensile strength of unwounded skin (Levenson et al. 1965, rat skin). — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4174176/
5. Human fetal skin wounds heal without a scar, regenerating a normal collagen weave and hair follicles, until about 22–24 weeks of gestation; after that they scar. — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3362931/
6. In adults, wounds of the oral mucosa (the lining of the mouth) heal faster than skin wounds and with minimal or nearly no scarring; reviews describe it as a rare example of nearly scarless postnatal healing in humans. — NIH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8394648/
7. On most of the body the epidermis is about 0.07–0.12 mm thick (about 0.1 mm on average), similar to a sheet of paper (≈ 0.1 mm); thinner on eyelids, up to about 1.5 mm on palms and soles. — anatomyatlases.org: https://anatomyatlases.org/MicroscopicAnatomy/Section07/Section07.shtml
8. PU.1 null mice, which lack macrophages and functioning neutrophils and so cannot mount the usual inflammatory response, repaired skin wounds on a similar time course to wild-type siblings, and the repair appeared scar-free, as in the embryo; inflammation is thought to be partly responsible for scarring ('one' = a scar). — research-information.bris.ac.uk: https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/wound-healing-in-the-pu1-null-mouse-tissue-repair-is-not-dependen/
```
