## Blockers

1. **Mislabelled mouth, 47.5–51.5 (factual).**
   - "your mouth: nearly the same" points into the **fetal skin** block, which is still tagged "fetal skin".
   - It sits under the badge "Illustration · ≈ 22–24 weeks", and the counter reads "still, inside / your mouth".
   - The screen therefore says the adult mouth lining is fetal skin, scarless before 22–24 weeks.
   - **Fix:** match-cut on "your mouth" to its own block: the oral lining (mucosa), badged "illustration · adult mouth lining". Show a cut closing with only a faint line, labelled "nearly scarless". Drop the fetal tag and the weeks badge at the cut.

2. **The mouse study contradicts its sentence, 61.8–67 (factual and honesty).**
   - The voice says mice **without** germ-fighting cells heal without a scar.
   - The picture shows those cells (white spheres) **gathering** at the seam of a **human** knee, framed as the UV camera on a person. The human daylight-tone strip sits underneath.
   - The badge "Mouse study · Martin et al., 2003" therefore sits on human skin, and the picture shows the cells present rather than absent.
   - **Fix:** show the mouse result on its own panel, badged "mouse study · Martin et al., 2003".
     - One option is a split: "with these cells: a seam" beside "without (mice): no seam".
     - Another is to show the cells absent and the wound closing with no seam, then "may be the price of defence".
     - Keep the knee and the daylight strip for the "So, why the scar?" line only.

3. **The micrograph is not always disclosed (honesty).**
   - **5.2–5.5:** the stained tissue shows at the top of frame during the dive. The note "BEHIND: A TISSUE PHOTO (NORMAL SKIN)" only fades in from about 5.53.
   - **59.4–59.7:** as the gauge fades, the micrograph fills the upper half of the frame with no note at all.
   - **Throughout:** the note is about 12 px mono caps (about 9 px cap height at 540 wide), which cannot be read on a phone.
   - **Fix:**
     - Tie the note's opacity to the micrograph's opacity, so it is on from the first visible frame to the last.
     - Raise the note to at least 15 px.
     - Fade the micrograph out before the gauge fades, not after.
   - **Also:** the micrograph is labelled "normal skin" yet sits behind the **fetal** illustration (39.1–51.5). That is disclosed but mixed. Either dim it under the fetal block or add "(adult)".

## (h) Production value against the "multi-million-dollar" bar

Each frame below reads as a default render, a template or a preset:

- **Lighting:** the weave and patch (5.6–39) and the fetal block (39–51.5) are lit flat. There is no visible rim, no volumetrics and no motivated key. The slab floats on a flat violet void with a hard, uniform edge.
- **Materials:** the collagen is uniform smooth tubes, like plasticine, and the patch is identical white bars. Neither has subsurface scattering, fibre sub-structure or wetness. The hook's terrain is the best tissue in the film, but its scar is a flat white strip.
- **Lens:** depth of field appears only in the hook and the rise. The section shots are pin-sharp everywhere, with no motion blur on the moves and no single grade: the warm hook, pink-plasticine section and lilac UV feel like three separate looks.
- **Detail and secondary motion:** the weave is static apart from the tug, with nothing drifting, no fibres breathing and no fluid. The immune "cells" (61.8–67) are untextured spheres.
- **Compositing:** the micrograph is a flat card behind a translucent glass plane, which reads as a template layer.
- **Type and HUD:** these do form one system and are the strongest part of the frame.

To reach 4:
- A three-point rig with a rim, plus a light volumetric haze in the section.
- Subsurface, fibre-detailed collagen with a slight sheen.
- Shallow depth of field and motion blur on every move, under one grade across all modes.
- Drifting particles and a slow breathing sway in the weave.
- Cells with membrane texture and a soft glow.
