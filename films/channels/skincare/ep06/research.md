# Tanning beds: research (Skin Sense s1v2)

## Strongest angles (one line each)

1. **The list ranks certainty, not size.** Tanning beds share IARC Group 1 with tobacco smoke, but the group says
   how sure the evidence is, not how big the harm; a 2026 Full Fact check caught a UK government publication
   blurring exactly that. (A wrong idea, with a callback built in.)
2. **Starting young.** The clearest signal in the evidence: first use before 35 goes with a melanoma risk about
   59% (Boniol 2012, corrected) to 75% (IARC 2007) higher in pooled studies. (Stakes.)
3. **A box brighter than noon.** Large, powerful units' UVA "may be 10 to 15 times" the midday sun's intensity
   (AAP 2011), though measured devices vary from about 2× to 26×. (A picture, but a soft number.)
4. **Countries switched them off.** Brazil banned sunbeds in 2009, months after IARC; Australia banned commercial
   solariums from 1 January 2015 (WA 2016). (Stakes at the scale of nations.)

## Chosen angle, and why it stops a scroll

**Angle 1, carried through 2–4:** "Same list as cigarettes?" is a thing people have heard and half-believe means
"just as deadly". The film refuses both the scare and the shrug: the list means the evidence is *sure*, and then it
walks through that evidence (the light, the young, the countries) to land on "not the same size of harm, but the
same certainty". It stops a scroll because the hook is a familiar claim the viewer can't quite judge, and the
answer is more precise than either side's version of it. It also keeps the film well clear of advice: it reports a
classification, measurements and two governments' decisions.

## Disputed or uncertain points

- **"Before 30" vs "before 35".** The brief's "75% before age 30" is IARC's 2009 public summary. The underlying 2007
  meta-analysis (Int J Cancer) reports RR 1.75 for first exposure **before 35**. The film voices neither age-75%
  pairing; it says "before thirty-five" and "more than half" (true of 1.59 and 1.75), with both on screen.
- **Boniol's 87% was wrong.** The published 1.87 came from a corrupted data file; the corrected figure is 1.59.
  Many websites still quote 87%. Don't use it. (The American Suntanning Association later disputed CDC's use of
  the 59% figure; CDC kept it, with clearer wording.)
- **The 10–15× figure is soft.** It is widely repeated and traceable to the AAP report (and, second-hand, to the
  FDA for high-pressure beds), but Cleveland Clinic calls it "older yet oft-cited research", and measured devices
  range widely: UVA 3–26× Oslo summer sun (Norway), 2–6× solar (Spain, 52 devices); WHO's own wording is
  "at least as intense as midday tropical sunlight". Kept hedged ("in large, powerful units", "may be"), badged on
  screen. **Dermatologist to decide whether to keep it or soften it to WHO's wording.**
- **Association, not a measured dose of harm.** The risk figures are relative risks from pooled observational
  studies; critics (e.g. Grant) argue confounding by skin type; IARC and later analyses held the association.
  Voiced as "went with", not "caused".
- **Group 1 is not a risk ranking.** Tobacco causes far more cancers than sunbeds (Cancer Research UK via Full Fact).
  The film says so; it never says sunbeds are "as dangerous as smoking".
- **Australia's date.** 1 January 2015 for most states and the ACT; Western Australia 1 January 2016. Voiced as
  "later".
- **Brazil's ban** has faced salon lawsuits; it stands nationally as far as the sources say.

## What a dermatologist must check

1. That nothing reads as advice (the film reports, never tells the viewer to use or avoid anything; bible §9).
2. The "ten to fifteen times" line: keep, or replace with WHO's "at least as intense as midday tropical sun".
3. "Raised by more than half" for first use before 35, and the on-screen pairing of 1.59 (Boniol, corrected) and
   1.75 (IARC 2007), with ages and "relative risk" badges.
4. "The sharpest signal came from people who started young" as a fair summary of the IARC evaluation.
5. "Reach past the whole top layer": UVA reaching the dermis (a share of it, not all).
6. "About as thin as paper" for the epidermis on most skin (≈ 0.1 mm).
7. "The same certainty": that it reads as IARC's evidence category, not certainty of harm to any one user.
