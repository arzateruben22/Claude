**Title**

Your Fingers Wrinkle on Orders From Your Nerves

**Description**

```
Why do fingers wrinkle? It's your nerves, not only the water.

Doctors have used this to test nerves for over eighty years. When the nerves to a finger stop working, the finger does not wrinkle. So the wrinkles are a sign of working nerves, not just soaking. So wrinkled fingers are your nerves at work. Whether they help you grip is still debated.

Series: Season 2: What your skin does, part 2 of 6. Next: how sweat really cools you

Sources
1. Water-induced finger wrinkling is an active process controlled by the autonomic nervous system. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23302867/
2. In 24 healthy adults, grade III finger wrinkling took a mean of 13.9 minutes in room-temperature filtered water. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40451152/
3. Stimulated skin wrinkling depends on digital vasoconstriction mediated via sympathetic nerve fibres; vasoconstriction is postulated to drive wrinkling through loss of digit volume, which pulls on the overlying skin. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25216595/
4. Skin wrinkling of the fingers after immersion depends on intact sympathetic innervation and is abolished by lesions of central and peripheral sympathetic pathways. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/420620/
5. A later study (n = 40) found wrinkling did not improve dexterity in handling wet objects nor affect touch sensitivity. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24416318/
6. Sweat evaporation from the skin surface plays a critical role in human thermoregulation. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31608304/
```
