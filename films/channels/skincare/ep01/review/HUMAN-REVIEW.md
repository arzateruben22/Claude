# What a person must check before this posts

## This episode
- **Claims, by a board-certified dermatologist.** Every line is in `CLAIMS-REVIEW.md`; all 16 rest on search excerpts,
  so each source is opened and ticked. Points to look at closely:
  - "The part you can see isn't [alive]: not one cell": the stratum corneum is dead corneocytes (c1); hair and nails
    are dead keratin too, so the line holds.
  - "More than a dozen deep" for about 15 rows: varies by site (about 6 on genital skin, far more on palms and soles).
  - "Rose for about two weeks": basal layer to stratum corneum; total turnover is often given as about 28 days and is
    longer with age (c6).
  - "Took itself apart. On purpose.": cornification as programmed cell death (Candi 2005; Lippens 2005).
  - "A kind of brick wall": the bricks-and-mortar model (badged "model"); sources credit Michaels et al. (1975) and
    Elias; no person is named aloud.
  - "Losing water about five times as fast": computed from Rutter & Hull's (1979) averages, about 32 g/m² an hour in
    babies under 30 weeks against about 6 near term; both figures and the study are on screen.
  - "Thinner than a fifth of a sheet of paper": 17 µm (cheek; Egawa 2007, 15 adults, one method) against about 100 µm.
  - "Kept alive" / "a layer that died for it": a reframe of the barrier's role in water balance; check it reads as
    figurative and not as a medical claim.
- **No advice, no diagnosis:** the film describes biology only. Confirm nothing reads as telling a viewer what to do.
- **Skin tones:** medium for the dive and the loop, deep for the two walls; nothing is said or implied about any tone,
  and the deep tone is not tied to prematurity (both walls share it).
