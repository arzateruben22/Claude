**Title**

Do they touch? | Meanwhile, Inside No. 004

**Description**

```
Do your nerve cells touch? For decades, no microscope could show it.

In the 1950s, electron microscopes finally showed it: a gap. At most junctions, electricity can't jump it. Calcium floods the tip, tiny sacs spill a chemical messenger, and it drifts across. So your nerve cells mostly don't touch. They talk, across gaps, in chemicals.

Series: Season 1: The traffic inside you, part 4 of 6. Next: Where breath goes

Sources
1. Conventional light microscopes resolve down to about 200 nm; the synaptic cleft (about 10–50 nm) is far smaller, so it could not be seen until electron microscopy in the 1950s. — nagwa.com: https://www.nagwa.com/en/videos/632148386161/
2. A neuron typically forms thousands of connections (synapses). — bri.ucla.edu: https://bri.ucla.edu/brain-fact/billions-of-neurons-trillions-of-synapses/
3. Camillo Golgi devised the silver stain used to see nerve cells (the 'black reaction', 1873) and supported the reticular theory: the nervous system as one continuous network. — scientifica.uk.com: https://www.scientifica.uk.com/neurowire/meet-the-nobels-golgi-and-cajal
4. Electron micrographs from 1954–55 (Palay and Palade; De Robertis and Bennett) showed the pre- and postsynaptic membranes separated by a cleft, confirming that neurons are separate cells. — rupress.org: https://rupress.org/jcb/article-pdf/168/1/12/1317505/jcb168112.pdf
5. At a chemical synapse the electrical signal does not pass to the next cell directly; it is converted into the release of a chemical neurotransmitter. (Electrical synapses, where current passes through gap junctions, also exist.) — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526047/
6. When the action potential reaches the terminal, voltage-gated calcium channels open and calcium entry triggers synaptic vesicles to fuse with the membrane and release neurotransmitter. — jove.com: https://www.jove.com/science-education/v/14896/chemical-synapses
7. Neurotransmitter is stored in synaptic vesicles; once released it diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds receptors on the next cell. The cleft is about 20–50 nm wide (JoVE), shown on the readout. — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11166/
8. Synapses can strengthen or weaken with activity (synaptic plasticity, e.g. long-term potentiation); the evidence supports plasticity being necessary for learning and memory, though not that it is sufficient. — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10845078/
9. A normal resting breathing rate for adults is about 12–20 breaths a minute; the tally uses 15 a minute. — lung.org: https://www.lung.org/blog/respiratory-rate-vital-signs
10. The breathing rhythm is generated in the brainstem (the pre-Bötzinger complex in the medulla); it reaches the diaphragm through spinal phrenic motor neurons, which make it contract at the neuromuscular junction, a chemical synapse. — neurosciencenews.com: https://neurosciencenews.com/breathing-pre-botc-neuroscience-4081/
```
