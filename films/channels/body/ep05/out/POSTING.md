Where breath goes | Meanwhile, Inside No. 005

**Description**

```
Where does your breath go? Not into a bag. Into these.

Each sac is wrapped in capillaries. Over much of it, the wall between air and blood is under half a micrometre: far thinner than a red cell. Oxygen slips across, no pump needed. Thicken that wall, and breathing gets harder. So your lungs aren't a bag. They're a sponge that spreads air thin.

Series: Season 1: The traffic inside you, part 5 of 6. Next: The exit

Sources
1. An adult's two lungs hold on average about 480 million alveoli (range 274–790 million in six lungs studied). — NIH: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14512270/
2. In Weibel's classic model the airways branch dichotomously through about 23 generations from the trachea to the alveolar sacs. — taylorandfrancis.com: https://taylorandfrancis.com/knowledge/Medicine_and_healthcare/Anatomy/Respiratory_zone
3. In the Galenic tradition the lungs were thought to be fleshy, made of coagulated (clotted) blood, and their function was held to be cooling the heart; Malpighi overturned both views. — scienzagiovane.unibo.it: https://scienzagiovane.unibo.it/English/scientists/malpighi-2.html
4. In 1661, in De pulmonibus (two letters to Borelli), Malpighi showed with a microscope that the lungs have a vesicular (bubble-like) structure, not a fleshy one; in a second letter, studying frog lungs, he described the capillaries. — encyclopedia.com: https://www.encyclopedia.com/doc/1G2-2830902791.html
5. The alveoli are surrounded by a dense network of capillaries where gas exchange takes place. — jove.com: https://www.jove.com/science-education/10884/
6. The thin portion of the air–blood barrier, about half of its surface, is under 0.5 µm thick, as thin as about 0.2 µm in places, against a red cell about 7.5 µm across (episode 1). — frontiersin.org: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/network-physiology/articles/10.3389/fnetp.2023.1142245/full
7. When the alveolar walls thicken and scar (as in pulmonary fibrosis), oxygen crosses into the blood less easily and people typically become short of breath, at first on exertion. — kids.frontiersin.org: https://kids.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frym.2021.582902
8. The alveolar (gas-exchange) surface of adult lungs is commonly given as about 70 m²; estimates run from about 50–100 m² (a standard physiology text) to 120–140 m² by morphometry. — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK493219/
9. A full (doubles) tennis court is 23.77 m × 10.97 m = 260.8 m² (ITF); 70 m² is about 27% of it, about a quarter. — itftennis.com: https://www.itftennis.com/media/7221/2026-rules-of-tennis-english.pdf
10. At rest an adult moves roughly 5–8 L of air a minute (about 500 mL a breath, 12–20 breaths a minute); the tally uses 5.5 L a minute. — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482502/
```
