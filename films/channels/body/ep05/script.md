# Meanwhile, Inside No. 05: "Where breath goes" (draft; a physiologist signs off before it posts)

About 70 s, 9:16, narrated by a synthetic voice (bf_emma, labelled as AI), with an original score.
Built from the bible's episode-5 row (`../bible.md`, §7) to the story standard
(`kits/cinema/script/WRITING.md`, including "The picture"). It pays off episode 4's tease ("Where does
all that air go?") and sets up episode 6 ("The exit").

- **The wrong idea and the person:** for centuries the lungs were thought to be solid flesh made of
  clotted blood, there to cool the heart; in 1661 Marcello Malpighi's microscope showed they are made of
  tiny air sacs.
- **The mechanism:** each sac wrapped in capillaries; a wall under half a micrometre over much of it;
  oxygen diffuses across, no pump.
- **The escalation and human scale:** unfolded, about seventy square metres (estimates run 50–140 m²),
  about a quarter of a full (doubles) tennis court.
- **An independent review (25 Oct, 6/10) fixed:** the hook no longer answers itself ("Not into a bag.
  Into these." plants the bag the takeaway corrects); the half-billion moves to the normal beat; "up to
  about twenty-three" (Weibel's idealised model); the old idea, Malpighi's lung and the barrier's and the
  area's wording (all changed again in the 26 Oct audit, below); 5.5 L/min for the tally (it reads 6.0 L
  on "six"); stakes in the why (a thickened wall); court lines in the whoa's shot. The channel keeps
  episode 1's beat order (takeaway, then the segment).
- **The 26 Oct audit (an independent fact check) changed:** "airy flesh" → "clotted blood" (the sources
  say the Galenic lung was fleshy, made of coagulated blood; "airy flesh" then "bubbles" sounded like the
  same idea); the lung under Malpighi's microscope → "an animal's lung" (the bubbles are his first
  letter's finding; the frog lungs are the second letter's, where he found the capillaries); "At its
  thinnest … about half a micrometre" → "Over much of it … under half a micrometre" (the thinnest parts
  are about 0.2–0.3 µm; about half the barrier is under 0.5 µm); "at least seventy" → "about seventy" (a
  textbook gives 50–100 m²; the screen now shows "estimates 50–140 m²"); "At least a third" → "About a
  quarter" of a tennis court (a full doubles court is 260.8 m², and the shot now draws one); the tally's
  5.5 L/min (already applied) checked.
- **Spine readout:** air sacs → airway splits → sac size → wall thickness → floor area → the tally.
