The jump | Meanwhile, Inside No. 003

**Description**

```
How fast is this spark? The leading expert doubted it could ever be timed.

It isn't current in a wire: the cell re-fires it, step by step. Many of your fibres wear fatty myelin, re-firing only at gaps, so the signal leaps. Where myelin is damaged, as in multiple sclerosis, signals can slow or stall. So a stubbed toe can hurt twice. The jolt rides faster fibres; the ache, bare ones.

Series: Season 1: The traffic inside you, part 3 of 6. Next: The gap

2. A nerve signal (action potential) travels along an axon, the long, thin projection of a single nerve cell, typically about a micrometre across. — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axon

6. An action potential is not carried passively like current in a wire (which would fade over distance); it is regenerated by voltage-gated ion channels in each successive patch of membrane, which is why conduction is far slower than electricity in a wire. — iu.pressbooks.pub: https://iu.pressbooks.pub/humanphys/chapter/action-potential-conduction/
7. Many vertebrate axons are wrapped in myelin, an insulating sheath of membrane layers made by glial cells (oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells), interrupted by bare gaps, the nodes of Ranvier. — NCBI (NIH): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10921/
```
