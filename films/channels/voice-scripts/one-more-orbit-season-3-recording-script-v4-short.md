# One More Orbit Season 3: your recording script (v4, your voice, short)

**Read this in your normal speaking voice: not a whisper.** Calm and a little amazed, like standing on a beach at night telling one friend what you see. This replaces the AI narration and is about **two thirds shorter** than the old script (380 words instead of 1,100): the pictures do most of the work now. Between your lines, the Moon visibly **grows, climbs, speeds up and fills the sky**, with a counter ticking down. Those moments are added in the edit, so just read your lines with a normal breath between them.

Season 3 is one long video, "How Close Can the Moon Get Before It Breaks?", cut into 8 short parts. Every fact below is the same checked fact as before; nothing new was added. If a phrase doesn't feel natural, say which line and it gets reworded and re-checked. The captions are rebuilt from your recording, so they'll match what you say.

**About 5 minutes of reading in one session.** You already recorded the closers ("Part one of eight…") for Possibility Lab; they're reused here, so there is no Part C to record.

### How to record
- **Room:** quiet, with soft things around you (a closet or bedroom; not a bathroom or kitchen).
- **Phone:** Voice Memos. Hold it **a hand's width (15–20 cm) from your mouth**, slightly to the side.
- **Pauses:** 2 seconds between lines. Flubbed one? Pause, then read **that whole line again**; the last take is kept.
- **Start each file by saying its name**, e.g. *"One More Orbit, long video"*.
- **Files:** `oo-s3.m4a` (Part A), `oo-s3-parts.m4a` (Part B), `oo-s3-cta.m4a` (Part D). One file is fine too: say each part's name before it.
- **Size:** GitHub takes files up to 25 MB. If a file is too big, set iPhone Settings › Apps › Voice Memos › Audio Quality to **Compressed**, or upload it in pieces like last time.
- **Upload:** https://github.com/arzateruben22/claude/upload/claude/space-s2/films/channels/space/voice-owner-spoken

### How it should sound
Quiet awe. Short lines, and let them land. The picture is the star, and your voice points at it.
- **Bold words** are the punch of the line: lean on them a bit. Say numbers slowly; they're the payoff.
- Lines marked *(cliffhanger)* end a part: a little slower, with a lift at the end.
- *[What you'll see]* notes are not read aloud. They show what's on screen while you're quiet.

## Part A. The long video
File: `oo-s3.m4a`. Read straight through, with a breath between lines.

**1.** Tonight, the Moon starts coming **closer**. And it never stops.
*[What you'll see: the Moon over the sea; the counter starts at 384,000 km]*

**2.** Right now it's about **three hundred eighty-four thousand** kilometers away, drifting off three point eight centimeters a year. Tonight, it reverses. A thought experiment, built from real physics.

**3.** Watch the number. It only goes **down**.
*[What you'll see: the counter falls; the Moon swells toward you]*

**4.** Two hundred eighty-eight thousand. A third **wider**. And the sea already feels it: more than **twice** the tidal pull.

**5.** *(cliffhanger)* Halve the distance, and the water starts to **climb**.

**6.** Half the distance. **Twice** as wide. **Eight** times the tidal pull.
*[What you'll see: hours squeezed into seconds; the waterline climbs the beach]*

**7.** Not a measured height. Just the idea: the whole ocean, lifted from above.

**8.** *(cliffhanger)* And the night is about to change.

**9.** One hundred fifty thousand. **Six and a half** times the moonlight. Your shadow is sharp on the sand.
*[What you'll see: the night brightens and your shadow sharpens]*

**10.** And it's **faster**. One lap takes about seven days, not twenty-seven.
*[What you'll see: a time-lapse; the Moon sweeps across the sky]*

**11.** *(cliffhanger)* It's bigger than the Sun now. So what happens when they **line up**?

**12.** One hundred thousand. Nearly **four** times wider. Its shadow on Earth is wider, and eclipses come far more **often**. Not every month, because the orbit is tilted. But often.
*[What you'll see: a huge black disc slides over the Sun; stars come out at noon]*

**13.** *(cliffhanger)* Closer. Faster. And the **ground** feels it too.

**14.** Seventy thousand. More than **a hundred sixty** times today's tidal pull. The solid ground already rises and falls tens of centimeters a day. Here, it would **flex** far more.
*[What you'll see: the ground swells and settles under the beach]*

**15.** *(cliffhanger)* Soon, the Moon rises on the **wrong** side of the sky.

**16.** Fifty thousand. Nearly **eight** times wider. One lap takes about a day and a quarter, close to how fast the Earth turns.

**17.** Forty-two thousand. It hangs **still**.
*[What you'll see: the Moon frozen in the sky; a small on-screen note: "orbit drawn over the equator"]*

**18.** Then it slides the wrong way. It rises in the **west**.
*[What you'll see: a moonrise in the west]*

**19.** Thirty thousand. Twenty-five. **Twenty**.
*[What you'll see: the Moon fills the sky and the counter races]*

**20.** Around eighteen thousand kilometers is the **Roche limit**: where Earth's tides pull harder than the Moon's own gravity holds it together. That's if the Moon acts like a fluid. A perfectly rigid Moon could come to about nine and a half thousand.

**21.** At eighteen thousand, it's more than **twenty** times wider than tonight. A wall of grey, straining.

**22.** *(cliffhanger)* And then it does something no Moon should **survive**.

**23.** It comes **apart**. Not an explosion. A **stretch**.
*[What you'll see: the near side pulls away; rock and dust stream along the orbit]*

**24.** The rock spreads along its old orbit, and over time it can flatten into a **ring**.
*[What you'll see: from the beach, a thin bright band crosses the sky]*

**25.** No Moon. Just a bright band, **horizon to horizon**.

**26.** The real Moon is still drifting away, three point eight centimeters a year. Tonight's Moon is **fine**.
