## 007 · Trees or stars
- **Wait, what?** There are more trees on Earth than stars in the Milky Way.
- **Payoff:** The galaxy's stars become trees one by one; Earth's forests overflow them. Trees win by roughly 8 to 30 times.
- **Claims:** (1) About 3.04 trillion trees, Crowther et al., *Nature* 2015: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14967
  (2) 100 to 400 billion stars, an estimate: https://science.nasa.gov/universe/exoplanets/our-milky-way-galaxy-how-big-is-space/
  (3) The star count is modelled from the galaxy's mass, not counted: https://asd.gsfc.nasa.gov/blueshift/index.php/2015/07/22/how-many-stars-in-the-milky-way/
  (4) The tree figure is itself a model with error bars (same paper).
- **Fit:** a comparison beats a count; the viewer's own backyard against the galaxy; reuses the galaxy block and tally sprites.

## 008 · Dark sky
- **Wait, what?** If space were endless and eternal, the night sky would blaze white.
- **Payoff:** A sightline of stars ramps toward white; a 13.8-billion-year limit switches the far ones off, so the dark is a clue to the universe's age.
- **Claims:** (1) Olbers' paradox: in an endless, eternal universe every line of sight ends on a star: https://www.britannica.com/science/Olbers-paradox
  (2) Universe age 13.787 ± 0.020 billion years (Planck 2018): https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Planck/Planck_science_highlights
  (3) Stars' finite lifetimes and the finite age together keep the sky dark: https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0007421 (check id when opening)
  (4) Edgar Allan Poe's 1848 guess at the answer, as a human beat (source to find).
- **Fit:** one famous puzzle, one honest answer; star-forest shot from the bible.

## 009 · Your gold
- **Wait, what?** The gold in a ring was made in a crash between dead stars.
- **Payoff:** Two neutron stars merge; the cloud cools and throws a bead of gold into a ring. Hedged: probably, mergers are one main source, and supernovae may add some.
- **Claims:** (1) The r-process makes about half the elements heavier than iron, gold among them: NASA Lawler PDF in the bible
  (2) GW170817's kilonova glowed from decaying heavy elements: https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/unfolding-story-of-kilonova-told-x-rays/
  (3) How much of Earth's gold came from mergers versus other events is still debated (source to find, e.g. a NASA or Caltech page).
  (4) Heavy elements were here before the Sun formed (the gold is older than Earth): source to find.
- **Fit:** the viewer's body and hand; follows ep07's galaxy scale; merger kit is new B3/B4 work.

## 010 · Black-hole edge
- **Wait, what?** At the edge of a black hole, escape needs more than light speed.
- **Payoff:** Split screen: the faller's clock runs on while the watcher sees it freeze at the horizon.
- **Claims:** (1) At the event horizon, escape speed equals c: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013300/a013322/black_hole_safety_brochure_web_final.pdf
  (2) Seen from afar, a fall appears to freeze at the horizon; at a supermassive hole you'd cross it before being torn apart (NASA 2024 visualization): https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/supermassive-black-holes/
  (3) The first direct image of a horizon-scale shadow, M87*, 2019 (Event Horizon Telescope): source to find.
- **Fit:** the season's big finale of the "speed limit" thread started in ep01.

## 011 · Saturn floats
- **Wait, what?** Saturn is less dense than water.
- **Payoff:** A bathtub-sized scale jump: Saturn dropped into a pool big enough would float, with the honest catch that it is gas and would not stay in one piece.
- **Claims:** (1) Saturn's mean density is about 0.687 g/cm³, water is 1.0: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/saturnfact.html
  (2) Saturn is mostly hydrogen and helium: https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/facts/
  (3) Saturn's equatorial diameter is about 9.4 Earths, mass about 95 Earths (same fact sheet).
  (4) A "float" is a thought experiment (our framing, labelled "analogy").
- **Fit:** a comparison with a kitchen object; reuses the planet and Sun-size blocks; no overlap with Sun size (that was a star).

## 012 · Footprints
- **Wait, what?** Footprints left on the Moon in 1969 are still there.
- **Payoff:** A close view of a boot print and the Apollo trails seen from orbit; no wind or rain to erase them, only a slow rain of dust grains.
- **Claims:** (1) NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter photographed Apollo landing sites with astronaut tracks: https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/lro-sees-apollo-landing-sites/ (find exact page)
  (2) The Moon has only a very thin exosphere, no weather: https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/
  (3) Erosion by micrometeorites is very slow, an estimate of millions of years for a print (source to find, hedge "could last").
  (4) Apollo 11 landed 20 July 1969: https://www.nasa.gov/mission/apollo-11/
- **Fit:** a human, emotional close to the season; ties to ep04's Moon and the viewer's "look up tonight".
