L20|Around eighteen thousand kilometers is the Roche limit: where Earth's tides pull harder than the Moon's own gravity holds it together. That's if the Moon acts like a fluid. A perfectly rigid Moon could come to about nine and a half thousand.
L21|At eighteen thousand, it's more than twenty times wider than tonight. A wall of grey, straining.
L22|And then it does something no Moon should survive.
L23|It comes apart. Not an explosion. A stretch.
L24|The rock spreads along its old orbit, and over time it can flatten into a ring.
L25|No Moon. Just a bright band, horizon to horizon.
L26|The real Moon is still drifting away, three point eight centimeters a year. Tonight's Moon is fine.
