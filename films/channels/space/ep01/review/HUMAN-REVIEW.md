## Every episode
1. **Claims.** An astronomer works through `CLAIMS-REVIEW.md`: every claim was checked from a search excerpt only, so a person opens each source link and ticks it. The episode does not post until every row is ticked.
2. **An astronomer's look at the pictures.** The episode README's "For a human to review" section lists everything shown but not claimed, and every honesty badge.
3. **A thought experiment.** The Sun cannot vanish; the film says "if" throughout. The galaxy (66–68 s) is badged "illustration". Earth's coastline is drawn from memory and is not a map.
4. **Units.** Kilometers only; decide whether to add miles for US viewers (a script change, so a new voice pass).
5. **The channel.** Light Minute and the narrator are provisional (`films/channels/DECISIONS.md`). Before the first post: the handle (@lightminute) on TikTok, YouTube and Instagram, and a USPTO/EUIPO trademark search (only open-web searches were done).
6. **AI labelling.** The narrator is synthetic (Kokoro). Turn on TikTok's AI-generated label and put "Voice: synthetic" in the post text; the same on Reels and Shorts where they ask.
7. **Sources in the post.** `python3 -I kits/cinema/script/lint.py <episode> --sources` prints the source list for the caption or a pinned comment.
8. **A listen.** Phone speaker and headphones: the voice over the score, the sound on each label and landing. Names to check: kilometers, gravitational, neutron, the stressed "us"; whether the tease at speed 0.79 sounds too slow.
9. **Cover frame.** The poster is 46.3 s (the Sun gone, Earth on its straight line) (`out/*-9x16.jpg`); TikTok lets you pick another in the app.
10. **Upload.** Upload the MP4 from the web or TikTok Studio, not through an app that re-compresses it. Leave auto-captions off: captions are burned in.
