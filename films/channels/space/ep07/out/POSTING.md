More trees, or more stars? Guess first.

There are more trees on Earth than stars in our galaxy. Big numbers hide in ordinary places.

Series: One More Orbit · Season 2 · Episode 1.

Sources
1. The Milky Way holds an estimated 100 to 400 billion stars (so 'hundreds of billions'); the exact range goes on screen. — science.nasa.gov: https://science.nasa.gov/universe/exoplanets/our-milky-way-galaxy-how-big-is-space/
2. A 2015 Nature study estimated about 3.04 trillion trees on Earth. — news.yale.edu: https://news.yale.edu/2015/09/02/seeing-forest-and-trees-all-3-trillion-them
3. The estimate combined ground-based tree-density measurements with satellite data and regression models. — news.yale.edu: https://news.yale.edu/2015/09/02/seeing-forest-and-trees-all-3-trillion-them
4. Both figures are estimates: the trees study 'estimates' 3 trillion+; NASA says the galaxy 'probably' holds 100 to 400 billion stars. — news.yale.edu: https://news.yale.edu/2015/09/02/seeing-forest-and-trees-all-3-trillion-them
5. 3.04 trillion / 400 billion = 7.6 and 3.04 trillion / 100 billion = 30.4, so roughly 8 to 30 times. — news.yale.edu: https://news.yale.edu/2015/09/02/seeing-forest-and-trees-all-3-trillion-them
6. Light needs about 100,000 years to cross the Milky Way's disc; written history is about 5,000 years. — science.nasa.gov: https://science.nasa.gov/universe/exoplanets/our-milky-way-galaxy-how-big-is-space/
7. Most of the Milky Way's mass is dark matter; the roughly 200 billion stars are a tiny percentage of it (NASA Hubble mass study). — science.nasa.gov: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/what-does-the-milky-way-weigh-hubble-and-gaia-investigate/
8. The 2015 estimate works out to about 420 trees per person (3.04 trillion over about 7.3 billion people at the time). — news.yale.edu: https://news.yale.edu/2015/09/02/seeing-forest-and-trees-all-3-trillion-them
9. Trees (3.04 trillion) outnumber the Milky Way's stars (100 to 400 billion) by roughly 8 to 30 times. — news.yale.edu: https://news.yale.edu/2015/09/02/seeing-forest-and-trees-all-3-trillion-them
