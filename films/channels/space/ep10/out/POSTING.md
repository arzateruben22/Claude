**Description**

```
What's at a black hole's edge? Look closer.

Time depends on where you are, and how strong gravity is: to a far-away watcher, a clock hovering near a black hole ticks slower. Both views are true.

Series: One More Orbit · Season 2 · Episode 4.

Sources
1. The event horizon is the boundary around a black hole beyond which nothing, not even light, can escape. — svs.gsfc.nasa.gov: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013300/a013322/black_hole_safety_brochure_web_final.pdf
2. Once you enter the event horizon, escaping requires traveling faster than light. — svs.gsfc.nasa.gov: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013300/a013322/black_hole_safety_brochure_web_final.pdf
3. Nothing can outrun light, so nothing escapes from within the horizon. — svs.gsfc.nasa.gov: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013300/a013322/black_hole_safety_brochure_web_final.pdf
4. Gravity slows clocks (gravitational time dilation); the nearer a mass, the slower a clock ticks. — nist.gov: https://www.nist.gov/atomic-clocks/a-powerful-tool-for-science/putting-einstein-test
5. GPS satellite clocks are corrected for relativity (gravity and speed), every day in the system's design. — nist.gov: https://www.nist.gov/atomic-clocks/a-powerful-tool-for-science/putting-einstein-test
6. Clocks closer to a mass tick more slowly than distant ones; so to a far-away watcher a clock hovering near a black hole runs slow, and the nearer it hovers, the bigger the difference (NASA brochure and NIST on gravitational time dilation). — svs.gsfc.nasa.gov: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013300/a013322/black_hole_safety_brochure_web_final.pdf
7. The Event Horizon Telescope released the first image of a black hole's shadow (M87*) in April 2019. — science.nasa.gov: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/first-image-of-a-black-hole/
8. M87* is about 55 million light-years from Earth. — science.nasa.gov: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/first-image-of-a-black-hole/
9. Escape velocity is the speed needed to break free of a body's gravity for good. — imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov: https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/black_holes1.html
10. For an observer near a black hole, time appears to pass faster for distant objects (NASA brochure: 'you feel like time is speeding up for everyone else'); stated for someone hovering near it, not for free fall. — svs.gsfc.nasa.gov: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013300/a013322/black_hole_safety_brochure_web_final.pdf
```
