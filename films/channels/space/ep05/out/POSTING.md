How big is the Sun | Light Minute No. 005

**Description**

```
Which holds a million Earths? They look like twins.

Nearly twenty-five centuries ago, in Athens, Anaxagoras said the Sun was a fiery stone, bigger than southern Greece. Calling it a stone, not a god, got him charged with impiety. Bigger, yes. By a factor of about 7,000. Astronomers even file the Sun under dwarf stars. Out there, this is what ordinary looks like.

Series: Season 1: How far, how fast, part 5 of 6.

Sources
1. Seen from Earth, the Sun and the full Moon have nearly the same angular size (about half a degree; on average the Sun about 32.0′, the Moon about 31.1′). — NASA: https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap080801.html
2. About 109 Earths would fit across the Sun's face. — NASA: https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/sun-compare/en/
3. Many sunspots are as large as Earth or larger (most 1,500–50,000 km across; a 2024 sunspot was almost 14 times Earth's size). — ESA: https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2004/08/A_typical_sunspot_and_Earth
4. Anaxagoras (c. 500–428 BCE), from Clazomenae, working in Athens in the mid-5th century BCE, described the Sun as a fiery mass (a red-hot stone or metal) larger than the Peloponnese, the southern peninsula of Greece. — iep.utm.edu: https://iep.utm.edu/anaxagoras/
5. Anaxagoras's claim ('larger than the Peloponnese') is true: the Sun (1,392,700 km across) is far larger. — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese
6. About 1.3 million Earths would fit inside the Sun (by volume). — NASA Science: https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/
7. The largest known stars are more than 1,000 times wider than the Sun (e.g. VY Canis Majoris, about 1,400–2,000 solar radii; Stephenson 2-18, about 2,150). — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_supergiant
8. The Sun is classed as a G2V star; the 'V' (main sequence) is the dwarf luminosity class, and it is popularly called a yellow dwarf. — livescience.com: https://www.livescience.com/space/the-sun/is-the-sun-really-a-dwarf-star
9. The Sun's diameter is about 1.39 million km: light crosses it in about 4.6 seconds. — solar-center.stanford.edu: https://solar-center.stanford.edu/vitalstats.html
10. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth. — howthingsfly.si.edu: https://howthingsfly.si.edu/ask-an-explainer/how-long-does-it-take-light-sun-get-earth
11. Earth orbits the Sun at about 29.78 km/s, so in the roughly 70 seconds before the tease you have travelled more than 2,000 km with it. — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html
12. The Sun accounts for about 99.8 percent of the mass in the solar system. — NASA: https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/its-surprisingly-hard-to-go-to-the-sun/
```
