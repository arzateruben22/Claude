## This episode
- **The history.** "For three hundred years" (Cassini 1666–67 to radar 1962); "the Cassinis, father and son,
  clocked about twenty-three hours" (Jacques Cassini's 23 h 15 min from his father's observations; c5);
  "Schiaparelli said one side always faced the Sun" (1890, a theoretical proposal; c6); "In nineteen sixty-two,
  radar from a dish in California pierced the clouds" (JPL Goldstone; Goldstein & Carpenter, Science 1963; the
  retrograde sense was confirmed by 1964; c7). A historian of astronomy may want primary sources: the ledger
  leans on IMCCE (Laskar) and Wikipedia.
- **"Faint smudges in those clouds."** Many of the old markings were probably illusions; ultraviolet photographs
  (1920s on) show real cloud features, which circle Venus in about four days, unrelated to its spin (c4's note).
- **"One turn outlasts a year" / "the day you'd live by is the Sun coming back."** The sidereal day (243.0 d) and the
  solar day (116.75 d) are both real days; the film says which is which (c9, c14).
- **"Rising in the west."** Nobody could see it from the surface through the clouds; it is the Sun's motion in
  Venus's sky (c13).
- **Readout:** "the radar's round trip, at its closest: about four minutes" = 2 × 38.2 million km / c = 255 s (c16).
- **Pronunciation at T0:** Schiaparelli (skya-pa-REL-li), Cassinis, "two hundred forty-three".
