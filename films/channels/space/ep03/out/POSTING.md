**Description**

```
Can one day outlast a year? On Venus: yes. And no.

In 1962, radar from a dish in California pierced the clouds. Both guesses were wrong. Venus turns once every 243 days, longer than its year. And it turns backwards. So yes: one turn outlasts a year. But the day you'd live by is the Sun coming back.

Series: Season 1: How far, how fast, part 3 of 6.

Sources
1. Earth's day (sunrise to sunrise) is 24 hours and its orbital period is 365.256 days, so you see the Sun rise about 365 times per orbit (Earth spins about 366.26 times relative to the stars). — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html
2. Venus is permanently shrouded in thick clouds (of sulfuric acid, 45–70 km up) that hide its surface from view. — NASA: https://solarsystem.nasa.gov/module/89
3. For about 300 years (Cassini, 1666–1667, to radar, 1962), astronomers tried to time Venus's rotation from faint, uncertain markings in its clouds. — perso.imcce.fr: https://perso.imcce.fr/jacques-laskar/en/general-audience/venus-spins-backward/
4. Giovanni Schiaparelli (1890) proposed that Venus is tidally locked to the Sun, keeping one face toward it (a rotation equal to its 225-day year). — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observations_and_explorations_of_Venus
5. Radar first measured Venus's spin at the 1961 conjunction (Goldstone, Jodrell Bank, Yevpatoria); Goldstone's 1962 run in California indicated a slow backward spin (about 230 ± 40–50 days), confirmed as retrograde in 1964 and refined to about 243 days by 1966. — NASA: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19630003286/downloads/19630003286.pdf
6. Venus's sidereal rotation period is 243.0 Earth days (radar, 2006–2020: 243.0226 days on average, varying by about 20 minutes), longer than its 224.7-day orbital period. — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html
7. On Venus the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east. — NASA: https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-venus/en/
8. An orbit is a fall: the Moon is always falling toward Earth while moving sideways fast enough to keep missing it. — NASA: https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-an-orbit-grades-5-8/
```
