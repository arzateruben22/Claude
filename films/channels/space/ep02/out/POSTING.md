How long to the nearest star? Say we leave today.

In 2012, it crossed into interstellar space. Nearly there, then? It hasn't even reached the Sun's cloud of comets. That's about 300 years away. Through it: maybe 30,000 more. So we've barely left for the stars. Voyager has crossed day one of 1,500.

Sources

3. Voyager 1 moves away from the Sun at about 17 km/s (about 38,000 mph). — NASA: https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/features/cosmic/nearest_star_info.html
4. The Moon's mean distance from Earth is 384,400 km; at Voyager's 17 km/s that takes about 6.3 hours. — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html
5. NASA gives the moment Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth (about 25.902 billion km, 173.14 AU) as 2:16:07 a.m. PST (10:16:07 UTC) on Wednesday 18 November 2026. — NASA Science: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1/voyager-1-what-is-a-light-day/
6. At one light-day, a command takes about a day to reach Voyager 1, and the team waits another day for its response. — kvia.com: https://kvia.com/cnn-other/2025/12/09/voyager-1-will-reach-one-light-day-from-earth-in-2026-heres-what-that-means/
7. Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause, the edge of the Sun's solar-wind bubble, into interstellar space on or about 25 August 2012 (announced September 2013), about 18.2 billion km from the Sun. — sciencenews.org: https://www.sciencenews.org/article/last-voyager-1-slips-interstellar-space
8. Voyager 1 has not yet reached the Oort Cloud, the theorised shell of icy bodies (comets) around the Sun, whose inner edge is thought to lie about 1,000–5,000 AU from the Sun (Voyager 1: about 173 AU). — NASA Science: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/oort-cloud/
9. Proxima Centauri, the nearest star to the Sun, is about 4.24 light-years away: about 1,549 light-days. — NASA Science: https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/other-stars-other-worlds/our-nearest-celestial-neighbor-an-exotic-3-star-system/
10. No Homo sapiens are known in Europe 75,000 years ago. Earlier claims did not last or are disputed: Apidima 1, Greece, at least 210,000 years (Harvati et al., Nature 2019; date and species disputed), and Grotte Mandrin, France, about 54,000 years (a short incursion; Neanderthals returned). 11. Venus takes 243.0 Earth days to spin once relative to the stars and 224.7 days to orbit the Sun: it turns slower than it orbits. — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html
