The falling Moon | Light Minute No. 004

**Description**

```
What's holding the Moon up? Nothing. It's falling.

The heavens, people believed, had their own rules. Newton, the story goes, wondered if the pull that drops an apple reaches the Moon. The Moon is sixty times farther from Earth's center than the apple. Gravity fades with distance squared: 3,600 times weaker. So the pull that drops an apple is the same pull that keeps the Moon falling.

Series: Season 1: How far, how fast, part 4 of 6.

Sources
1. Nothing holds the Moon up: no force pushes it away from Earth; gravity pulls it toward Earth all the time. — NASA: https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-an-orbit-grades-5-8/
2. The Moon is constantly falling toward Earth: it falls about 1.36 mm below its straight-line path every second. — astronomy.ohio-state.edu: https://www.astronomy.ohio-state.edu/martini.10/Astro171_Win07/Unit4/lecture17.html
3. The Moon's mean distance (384,400 km) is about 30 Earth diameters (12,742 km). — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html
4. New York City to Boston is about 306 km in a straight line: about 5 minutes at 1.022 km/s. — geodatos.net: https://www.geodatos.net/en/distances/from-new-york-city-to-boston
5. With no net force acting, a moving object keeps moving at constant speed in a straight line (Newton's first law). — NASA: https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/BGP/newton1.html
6. By John Conduitt's account, Newton in 1666, in the garden at his mother's home, reasoned that the power that brings an apple down might reach 'as high as the Moon', and if so might keep her in her orbit (a later anecdote, hence 'the story goes'). — todayinsci.com: https://todayinsci.com/C/Conduitt_John/ConduittJohn-Quotations.htm
7. Gravity weakens with the square of distance, so at 60 times the distance from Earth's centre it is 60² = 3,600 times weaker. — homepages.uc.edu: https://homepages.uc.edu/~hansonmm/ASTRO/LECTURENOTES/F03/Gravity/Page1.html
8. Dropped from rest, an object at Earth's surface falls about 4.9 m in the first second; the Moon falls about 4.9 m below its straight line in one minute. — web.pa.msu.edu: https://web.pa.msu.edu/courses/2008fall/AST207/09-15.pdf
9. Moonlight takes about 1.3 seconds to reach Earth, so you always see the Moon as it was about 1.3 seconds earlier. — NASA: https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/light-year/en/
10. In Aristotle's physics, followed for centuries, the heavens beyond the Moon were made of a different element (aether) moving naturally in circles, with different rules from falling bodies on Earth; Newton's universal gravitation united the two. — people.wku.edu: https://people.wku.edu/jan.garrett/103/aris_to_modsci.htm
```
