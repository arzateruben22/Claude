Your speed | Light Minute No. 006

**Description**

```
How fast are you moving now? Sitting perfectly still.

Ptolemy argued that if Earth moved, birds and clouds would be left behind. In 1632, Galileo answered with a ship's cabin. Below deck on calm water, nothing tells you the ship is moving. You feel a jolt, never a smooth ride. So your speed has no single answer. First ask: compared to what?

Series: Season 1: How far, how fast, part 6 of 6.

Sources
1. At the equator, Earth's rotation carries the surface at about 1,670 km/h (NASA Space Place gives 1,675 km/h). — NASA: https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/launch-windows/en/
2. Sound travels about 1,225 km/h in air at sea level and 15 °C, so 1,670 km/h is faster than sound. — NASA: https://www.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/atoms/files/speed_of_sound_9-12.pdf
3. Earth's mean orbital speed is 29.78 km/s. — NASA: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html
4. At 29.78 km/s Earth covers more than 306 km (New York to Boston in a straight line) in the time from the film's start to this line. — geodatos.net: https://www.geodatos.net/en/distances/from-new-york-city-to-boston
5. Ptolemy (Almagest I.7) argued against Earth's motion: if Earth moved through space, things not attached to it would be left behind; and if it spun, clouds, birds in flight and thrown objects would not keep up with it (he aims the clouds-and-birds point at the spin). — origin-www.alibris.com: https://origin-www.alibris.com/Historical-Survey-of-the-Astronomy-of-the-Ancients-Sir-George-Cornewall-Lewis/book/2930164
6. In his Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems (1632), Galileo described the cabin below decks of a large ship: flies, butterflies, fish in a bowl and dripping water behave the same whether the ship is at rest or moving smoothly. — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo%27s_ship
7. The Sun moves around the centre of the Milky Way at about 828,000 km/h (about 230 km/s; modern estimates about 220–250 km/s). — NASA Science: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/overview/
8. About 230 million years ago, the first dinosaurs had just appeared (the earliest known date to about 231–233 million years, Late Triassic). — amnh.org: https://www.amnh.org/explore/videos/dinosaurs-and-fossils/150-million-years-of-dinosaur-evolution
9. The Sun is about 27,000 light-years from the galactic centre (8,275 pc), so light from there takes about 27,000 years to reach us. — mpe.mpg.de: https://www.mpe.mpg.de/6588951/The-black-hole
```
