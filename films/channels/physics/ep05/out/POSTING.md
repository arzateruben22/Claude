**Description**

```
Why does it speed up? Nobody pushes. Nothing motorized.

It feels like something pushed. Nothing outside did. With no twist from outside, your total amount of spin can't change. Weight near the middle is easier to turn, so the same amount turns you faster. Your arms paid for every extra turn. One push set your total spin. Your arms just decide how fast.

Series: Season 1: Forces you can see, part 5 of 6.

Sources
1. Pulling two 2 kg dumbbells (and the arms holding them) from 0.8 m to 0.2 m from the axis while seated on a freely turning chair raises the spin rate by about 2.2–3.1 times (2.68 in the scene's model). — OpenStax: https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/11-3-conservation-of-angular-momentum
2. A mass m at distance r adds m·r² to the moment of inertia, so weight pulled toward the axis is easier to turn; with the angular momentum fixed, ω = L/I rises. — OpenStax: https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/10-4-moment-of-inertia-and-rotational-kinetic-energy
```
