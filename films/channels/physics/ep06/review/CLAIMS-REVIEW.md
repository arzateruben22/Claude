| c2 | Nothing rises. | With a glass filled to the brim and a rigid lid sealed around the straw, no air presses on the drink and water barely compresses, so sipping lowers the pressure in the straw but lifts nothing: a straw needs air pressing on the drink. | [Southeast Missourian, 'Straw science' (Hooked on Science column)](https://www.semissourian.com/features/straw-science-1464828) (accessed 2026-10-26) | search excerpt; status ok | ☐ | Excerpt (the jar-lid version of the demonstration: a straw sealed into a jar lid with clay): 'When you suck on the straw you lower the air pressure in the straw. The mechanism: IPM's A Moment of Science (https://www.ipm.org/show/amomentofscience/2023-11-21/air-pressure-and-drinking-straws), search excerpt: a straw works by atmospheric pressure, not suction. Reasoning (ours): full to the brim, there is no air in the glass to expand, and water's compressibility (about 4.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ per Pa) lets a hard sip (about 10 kPa) draw in only about five millionths of the glass's volume; the pressure in the glass stays far above water's vapor pressure, so nothing boils or rises. The earlier staging (a jar pumped empty around an open straw) was dropped in review: room air would have pushed down the straw and bubbled out of its foot. On screen: 'Rigid lid, no air inside'. |
