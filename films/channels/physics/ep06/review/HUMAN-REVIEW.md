## This episode
1. **Claims.** A physicist works through `CLAIMS-REVIEW.md`: every claim was checked from search excerpts only, so a person opens each link and ticks it.
2. **The heights are computed, the climb's pace isn't.** h = Δp/ρg with ρ = 998.2 kg/m³ and g = 9.81 m/s²: the sip lifts the drink 19.3 cm (19 hPa); a perfect pump leaves water's own vapour (23.4 hPa at 20 °C), so the tall column stops 10.11 m above the basin, and the measure's 10.3 m is the column that weighs as much as the air above it (1.03 kg per cm²). How fast the tall column climbs is an illustration, badged "Climb speed: illustration".
3. **The jar (the change).** It is pumped to 100 hPa, below anything a mouth can reach inside the straw (about 900 hPa at best), so the straw's own air pushes the drink out of it and no sip lifts it; 100 hPa is well above water's vapour pressure, so the drink does not boil there. This is reasoned from the straw mechanism (c2), not quoted from a source.
4. **"Your lungs? About one meter."** Typical maximal inspiratory pressure is 70–125 cm of water (men about 100, women about 70), so "about one meter" is the typical adult's best; many people manage less.
5. **Torricelli.** The quotation ("we live submerged at the bottom of an ocean of air", letter to Ricci, 11 June 1644) is paraphrased, not quoted on screen. Some accounts credit Viviani with performing the experiment; the line only says Torricelli "saw why".
6. **Spelling.** The narration and captions use US spelling ("meters", "centimeters") like episodes 2–5; the title and episode 5's end card use the bible's "ten-metre", and episode 5's tease said "storeys". Provisional; listed in PRODUCTION.md's questions.
7. **Honesty notes on screen:** "Illustration" (Torricelli's tube, the house), "Climb speed: illustration", "Water at 20 °C", "At sea level".
8. **The spine:** the air in the straw (1013 → 994 hPa on the sip) → the air around the glass (1013 → 100 hPa as the jar is pumped) → the air on the drink (1013 hPa) → the water column (0 → 10.11 m) → your lungs' best (1.0 m) → the air on 1 cm² of drink (1.03 kg) → the straw again (994 hPa).
