# Reality Rulebook Season 3: your recording script (Part 1, your voice)

**The vibe: engaging and fun, like teaching a friend who's about to say "wait, WHAT?"** Smile while you talk (it's
audible), let the numbers land, and give the reveals a beat of suspense. Curious and playful, never shouty or salesy.

This is Part 1, "Watch a wing, slowed down": a hummingbird at **10× slower**, then halfway we slow time **again to 20×**
so you can see the wing's figure-eight. On screen, a little clock slows down and a speed tag flips each time the speed
changes. Every fact is the same checked fact as before; the one new number (a fly's ~200 wingbeats a second) gets
checked before posting.

**About 3 minutes of reading.** If a line doesn't feel natural in your mouth, change the wording a little: the captions
are rebuilt from your recording.

### How to record
- **Room:** quiet, with soft things around you (a closet or bedroom; not a bathroom or kitchen).
- **Phone:** Voice Memos, **a hand's width (15–20 cm) from your mouth**, slightly to the side.
- **Pauses:** 2 seconds between lines. Flubbed one? Pause, then read **that whole line again**; the last take is kept.
- **Start the file by saying its name**: *"Reality Rulebook, part one"*.
- **Files:** one file is fine: `rr-s3-part1.m4a`. Say "closers" before Part C and "like and follow" before Part D.
- **Size:** GitHub takes files up to 25 MB. If it's bigger, set iPhone Settings › Apps › Voice Memos › Audio Quality to
  **Compressed**, or upload it in pieces.
- **Upload:** https://github.com/arzateruben22/claude/upload/claude/physics-s2/films/channels/physics/voice-owner-spoken

### How it should sound
- **Bold words** are the punch: lean on them. Say numbers a touch slower; they're the payoff.
- **…** means a tiny suspense pause before a reveal.
- *(cliffhanger)*: a little slower, with a lift at the end, like you're teasing what's next.
- *[What you'll see]* notes are not read aloud.

## Part A. Part 1: "Watch a wing, slowed down"

**1.** *(the hook: grab them)* This bird flaps its wings **fifty** times a second. Let's actually *see* it.
*[What you'll see: a hummingbird at the window feeder, wings a total blur. Tag: 1× real time]*

**2.** Quick heads-up: no camera could film everything in this series, so every picture here is drawn by a computer, straight from the **physics**.

**3.** Here's a hummingbird at full speed. The wings? Total **blur**.

**4.** So let's slow time down. **Ten** times slower. One second now takes ten.
*[What you'll see: the clock slows, the tag flips to 10× slower]*

**5.** Fifty beats a second becomes **five**. And look… there they are. Up, and down.

**6.** Okay, let's go further. **Twenty** times slower.
*[What you'll see: the clock slows again, the tag flips to 20× slower; for 2 seconds the screen splits, 10× left, 20× right]*

**7.** Now every single beat takes almost **half a second**.

**8.** Watch the very tips of the wings. See that? A tiny **figure-eight**, flipping over at each end.
*[What you'll see: a faint trail drawn by the wing tips]*

**9.** So why the blur at full speed? Your eyes blend anything faster than about **sixty** flashes a second. Fifty beats? Just mush.

**10.** But check out the fly next to the cup. Twenty times slower… and its wings are **still** a blur.
*[What you'll see: the fly by the cup, wings smeared]*

**11.** *(cliffhanger)* To freeze those, we're gonna need a **hundred** times slower.
